WORK: Sāṃkhyatattvavivecana  (slug: simananda-samkhyatattvavivecana-69b94a)

CHAPTER: 14-twenty-eightfold-incapacity



[0] **Tats_13**

[13] [Base sūtra:] Incapacity (aśakti) in twenty-eight ways.

[70] **Tats_13.c1**

[86] [Verse:] Dullness [of hearing], leprosy [of skin], blindness [of sight], [loss of] taste, [loss of] smell, dumbness, lameness, withered-arm, anal disorder, impotence, and madness—this [list of injuries to the organs] is thus set forth.

[323] **Tats_13.c2**

[339] [Verse:] The injury of the eleven organs—in the form of deafness and the rest—[gives eleven]; the injury of the intellect is seventeenfold, [consisting of] the inversions of the {{tuṣṭi / siddhi / aśakti|contentments and attainments}} [i.e. nine inverted contentments and eight inverted attainments].

[641] **Tats_13.c3**

[657] [Verse:] The conviction "there is no {{pradhāna|primordial nature}}" is [the inverted contentment called] "ajambhikā"; "there is no great principle" is [the inversion of] "salila"; and "āyācchā" [is inverted] in [the denial] "there is no I-maker";

[906] **Tats_13.c4**

[922] [Verse:] or "there is no unseen [merit]"—[the denial that] the pentad of subtle elements is the cause of the [gross] elements. "All this is just brahman; being brahman alone, one attains brahman"—as is the view of the Vedāntins, so [erroneously, here is held to be] such an opinion of [these false] Sāṃkhyas.

[1232] **Tats_13.c5**

[1248] [Verse:] Engagement in acquiring objects [is the inverted external contentment "asutā"]; engagement in safeguarding [them is] "sutarā"; engagement in [amassing] wealth by one who does not see the fault of acquisition [is] "asunetrā"—so it is proclaimed by the Sāṃkhyas.

[1519] **Tats_13.c6**

[1535] [Verse:] "Akurmarīcikā" is the capacity for enjoyment of one who does not see violence and the rest [as faults]; "anuttambhāmbhasikā" is the engagement in [worldly] water [i.e. activity] on account of the enjoyment of faults.

[1762] **Tats_13.c7**

[1778] [Verse:] These nine are declared to be the inversions of contentment. Now the eight attainments [in inversion] are stated; these are the inversions of the attainments:

[1947] **Tats_13.c8**

[1963] [Verse:] For one who reasons toward plurality, the establishing of the One is [the inverted attainment] "atāraka"; misapprehension from [mere] hearing of words is "asutāraka";

[2140] **Tats_13.c9**

[2156] [Verse:] Having heard "the knower of plurality is liberated," the knower of unity is not a sharer in liberation—[this is the inverted "tāra"]. Through entanglement in ignorance and bad scriptures that do not carry one across; and [the inverted] "apramodā" is, in transmigration, the absence of agitation even in one who suffers.

[2486] **Tats_13.c10**

[2503] [Verse:] "Apramuda," or that "apramodamāna" [which is being] gladdened by ignorance—[arising] either from incorrect instruction or from obstruction in regard to the teacher;

[2678] **Tats_13.c11**

[2695] [Verse:] for the unfortunate there would be no attainment in knowledge—so it is declared. "Aramyaka" is the [relapse into] ignorance after knowledge, through affectionate association [with the unworthy].

[2900] **Tats_13.c12**

[2917] [Verse:] These eight inversions of attainment have been explained in the foregoing manner. Thus incapacity in twenty-eight ways has been explained by the combination of both [the eleven organ-injuries and the seventeen intellect-injuries].
