CHAPTER: 12-book-12



[0] 12. In the twelfth delight your heart, where as a new flower of the Loves Ampelos sent up his form into the vine-clad fruit.

[126] So they, beside the brows of western Ocean,

[171] were sailing to the halls of the generative Sun.

[221] And as they journeyed the star Hesperus met them,

[272] leaping from the hall; and hastening forth herself,

[325] newly appeared, rose Selene, driver of oxen.

[371] And before the eyes of the life-bearing Charioteer

[423] they bent their fruitful track. He, having just finished his course,

[493] returned from the air; and the radiant Morning Star

[546] set down, near the four-horse car of the fiery-eyed driver,

[607] the hot harness and the starry whip,

[645] cleansing beside the streams of neighbouring Ocean

[697] the bodies of the fire-fed horses, dripping with sweat;

[754] and the colts, shaking the wet manes of their necks,

[808] stamped with gleaming hooves in the glowing manger.

[861] And the daughters of Chronos, around the fiery throne,

[917] flying in a circle about the unwearied Charioteer,

[969] the twelve circling Hours greeted the four,

[1014] handmaids of the Sun, companions of the glowing car,

[1068] ministers of Lycabas in turn; for to the primeval

[1119] ruler of the whole universe they bent a slavish neck.

[1174] And the grape-tending Hour cried out a word to him,

[1227] holding the autumnal sickle as witness of her supplication:

[1288] "Sun, grain-giver, planter, lord of fruits,

[1333] when will the vineyards grow the wine-bearing cluster?

[1389] And to which of the blessed does Aion betroth this honour?

[1449] Yes, I beseech you, do not hide it, for I alone

[1498] of all my sisters am without honour; for I grow no fruit,

[1557] no ear of grain, no meadow, and not the rain of Zeus."

[1613] She spoke; and the Sun encouraged the nurse

[1658] of the fruit to be, and beside the opposite wall

[1708] raising his finger he showed the circling girl

[1756] the unequal tablets of Harmony, on which there lie

[1808] in one all the oracles, all that were ordained for the world

[1870] that the prophetic hand of first-born Phanes inscribed,

[1927] and embroidered with his pen the fitting house for each.

[1985] And Hyperion, the steward of fire, spoke a word:

[2035] "On the third tablet, whence the wine-fruit shall come,

[2092] you will learn, where the Lion and the Virgin are; and on the fourth,

[2163] who is the sceptred lord of the cluster, where, drawing sweet nectar,

[2234] with painted hand Ganymede lifts the cup."

[2278] As the god spoke thus, the vine-loving girl ran,

[2328] turning her eyes about, and beside the wall of the oracle

[2387] she beheld the first tablet, coeval with the endless world,

[2448] bearing in one all things, all that the sceptred Ophion

[2505] accomplished, all that aged Cronos achieved, when, cutting

[2565] his father's male plough-share, he ploughed the bridal water,

[2628] sowing the unsown back of the daughter-bearing sea,

[2681] who once received his stone son in his gaping throat,

[2736] feasting on the counterfeit body of the lying Zeus;

[2789] and the stone delivered the brood of the children hidden within,

[2855] casting forth the burden of the pregnant throat.

[2905] But when the storm-footed Hour, the handmaid of Phaethon,

[2964] beheld the fire-bright victory of Zeus as he fought

[3017] and the hailing war-strife of the Cronian snow,

[3066] she looked in turn at the neighbouring tablet; and that one held

[3132] how the pine bore mortal offspring in travail, or whence suddenly,

[3200] unfolding its tree-like, generative childbearing,

[3251] the pine cast up an unsown, self-made son,

[3295] and whence rain-bringing Zeus flooded all the cities,

[3350] bringing the heaped-up water in towering seas,

[3398] how Notus from Boreas, and Eurus from Libs scourging,

[3453] lifted the wandering ark of Deucalion, neighbour of the Moon,

[3516] into an air-walking voyage, without a harbour.

[3564] And when the minister of Lycabas ran over the third tablet

[3624] with well-footed sole, the coiling girl halted,

[3673] gazing at the fated, many-turning oracles of the world,

[3730] reddening letters, inscribed with wise vermilion,

[3781] all that the various-storied, primeval mind had written,

[3839] such things prophesying, and she read on the tablets:

[3894] Argus, herdsman of Hera, will change into the shape of a bird,

[3958] keeping the bright form of his grim eyelids; but also

[4013] Harpalyce herself, after the bed of sinful unions,

[4065] having butchered her son for her daughter-wedding father,

[4124] will row her airy, winged journey

[4159] as a storm-like bird; and the web-toiling Philomela

[4212] will be a swallow with a spangled neck, twittering,

[4265] crying out the testimony of her tongueless silence,

[4318] having written speaking devices on a wise tunic;

[4368] and Niobe, beside the foot of Sipylus, a stone with sense,

[4428] lamenting with stony tears the rank of her children,

[4482] will stand as a piteous statue. And there will be a neighbour,

[4546] Pyrrhus, a Phrygian stone, love-maddened, still holding for Rhea

[4612] a lawless goad of unwedded unions,

[4648] and Thisbe a moist water, and Pyramus, both coevals,

[4702] longing for each other; and of the fair-crowned girl

[4756] Smilax, Crocus, longing, will be a flower of the Loves;

[4813] and after the bridal turning-post of the storm-footed weddings

[4877] and after the apples of the Paphian, into the shape of a lion

[4940] Artemis will goad transformed Atalanta.

[4981] And the restless Hour passed over all these things in one,

[5041] until she came to the place where fiery Hyperion

[5091] declared the symbols of prophecy to the storm-like girl,

[5149] where the Lion was wrought, radiant, where also

[5198] the starry Virgin herself was embroidered in a counterfeit shape

[5264] holding the wine-dark cluster, the summer-born flower of the fruit;

[5333] there the daughter of Chronos rested her feet, and read this:

[5396] Cissos, the high-flying, lovely youth, creeping into a plant,

[5459] will be the coiling ivy even among shoots; and of the youth

[5520] Calamus the upright reed, bent by the breezes,

[5568] will appear a thin shoot of the growth-giving field,

[5622] a prop of the vines; and changing his form into a plant,

[5680] Ampelos gives his name to the vine-clad fruit.

[5728] But when the harvest-girl beheld these oracles,

[5777] she sought that place, where beside the neighbouring wall

[5836] the image of Ganymede was inscribed with a fashioned form,

[5896] dripping the nectar-moisture from a golden cup,

[5945] where was the four-fold voice of the inscribed words;

[6000] there the grape-loving goddess reveled, and the nymph found

[6061] these oracles kept for ivy-bearing Lyaeus:

[6105] Zeus granted to Phoebus to have the prophetic laurel,

[6160] and reddening roses to rosy-skinned Cyprogeneia,

[6210] the grey-green olive-shoot to grey-eyed Athena,

[6259] and ears of grain to Demeter, and the vines to Dionysus.

[6317] Such things did the Bacchus-loving girl behold in the writings;

[6382] and rejoicing she darted off, and taking her sisters

[6436] she passed into the stream of eastern Ocean,

[6482] running together with the horsemanship of Phaethon.—But for Lyaeus

[6550] there was no remedy, with his comrade fallen, nor was there

[6611] memory of the dance; but with his mind shaken by the goad of love

[6678] he sang bitter dirges, and in careless silence

[6726] he left the bronze back of the unsounding ox-hide;

[6778] nor did the lyre delight him. And as, with mirthless face,

[6838] loving Dionysus piteously wailed,

[6873] the stream of the Lydian, reedy Hermus was stayed,

[6925] though it rolled swiftly with the windy beat of its current,

[6987] and longed not to flow; and with its deep-rich stream

[7042] saffron Pactolus reined back its mournful water,

[7092] bearing the likeness of a downcast man; and about the corpse

[7154] Sangarius checked the back-rushing draught of its spring channels,

[7222] pouring its Phrygian stream; and the breathless image

[7277] of the Tantalid, ill-bearing mother, drenched by the groanings,

[7342] shed double tears, as Dionysus lamented;

[7384] and the pine, wailing, companion of its coeval fir,

[7437] whispered faintly; and the laurel herself,

[7481] being the tree of unshorn Phoebus, shook off her hair

[7536] in the mournful winds; and the sleek, unpruned olive

[7590] poured its leaves to the ground, even though it was Athena's plant.

[7659] So, as tearless Dionysus groaned with longing,

[7707] the dread, unbound threads of the Fate were turned again;

[7766] and consoling the wail of grieving Dionysus

[7811] steadfast-worded Atropos cast up an inspired voice:

[7864] "Your youth lives, Dionysus, and he will not cross

[7916] the bitter water of Acheron; for your wail has found a way to undo

[7984] the unbending, recoverable threads of unswerving Fate;

[8040] Ampelos has not died, even though he died; for into a lovely

[8102] drink, into sweet nectar, I will change your boy;

[8153] him, with the dancing beat of the well-rolling hand,

[8207] the double-noted pipe, beating out the harmony of the feast,

[8269] will hymn, holding a Phrygian rhythm or a Dorian song;

[8325] or in the theatres a rhythmic man will sing of him,

[8378] pouring the Ismenian echo of the Aonian reed,

[8425] or to the dwellers of Marathon; and the Mysians will cry "Euoi"

[8490] for lovely Ampelos along with vine-clad Lyaeus.

[8539] And leaving the crooked, serpent girdle of your locks

[8594] you will wreathe clusters of grapes about your hair,

[8648] bringing envy to Phoebus, because he wields with his hand the mournful

[8720] tree-borne dirges of the weeping hyacinths,

[8765] while you have a drink, the rest of the mortal race,

[8819] the earthly likeness of heavenly nectar, and your flowery youth

[8884] will put to shame the boast of the Amyclaean boy;

[8935] and if that one's city raises warlike bronze,

[8982] your youth's land too will grow, light-bringing,

[9032] the moist rain of the reddening river-dew,

[9076] luxuriant wholly with gold, and rejoicing not in iron;

[9132] if it glories about the stream of a sounding river,

[9185] the Pactolian water is better than the Eurotas.

[9234] Ampelos, you gave grief to griefless Dionysus,

[9282] that, when your honey-dropping wine is grown,

[9329] you might give delight to the whole four-fold world

[9382] and a libation to the blessed and gladness to Dionysus;

[9439] lord Bacchus wept, that he might loose the tears of mortals."

[9502] So speaking the goddess went, companion of her sisters.

[9559] And a great wonder appeared to mournful Bacchus, the witness;

[9622] for the lovely corpse, springing up like a creeping serpent,

[9684] Ampelos, self-made, changed his own shape,

[9728] and became a sweet flower; and as the corpse was transformed

[9790] his belly was a tall bush, and the tips of his hands

[9844] put forth branches, and his feet were rooted,

[9891] his curls were grape-clusters, and the very fawnskin

[9945] was shaped into a many-figured flower of the growing fruit,

[10006] and the vine-cluster was his long-shadowed neck,

[10056] and like to his elbow the crooked tendril stretched,

[10110] swelling with grapes, and as his head was transformed

[10165] the bent, curved clusters mimicked the shape of his horn.

[10224] There were endless rows of plants; and self-made

[10274] the vine-clad row, coiling its green tendrils,

[10322] girt the neighbouring trees with the new wine-dark fruit.

[10381] And there was a new wonder, since the boy at play then,

[10438] coiling his slanting foot about a high-leaved plant,

[10492] high-flying Cissos made his form into a tree,

[10539] and became a curved shoot named for him, and girt

[10590] the newly grown row of vines with a crooked bond.

[10641] And with friendly leaves he shaded the overshadowed temple,

[10702] and intoxicated his locks from the wine-loving leaves,

[10758] Dionysus exulting; and of the growing boy

[10801] he plucked the fruit of the harvest just now ripening.

[10857] And the god, self-taught, without foot, outside the wine-press,

[10922] pressing the cluster with the heavy fruit of his palm,

[10978] squeezing the travail of drunkenness with entwining hands,

[11038] revealed the new-flowing mass of the purple fruit,

[11090] and found the sweet drink; the white fingers of wine-pouring

[11152] Dionysus reddened as they were drenched.

[11194] And he held a curved cup, the horn of an ox; and of the sweet-drinking

[11266] dew Bacchus tasted with the tips of his lips,

[11313] tasted also of the fruit, and delighting his heart with both

[11375] he cast up a word from his proud throat:

[11417] "Ambrosia and the nectar of my Zeus, Ampelos, you bring forth;

[11481] bearing two beloved shoots, the fruit, Apollo

[11528] ate not of the laurel, and drank not from the hyacinth;

[11585] no ear of grain travails a sweet drink; be gracious, Deo;

[11644] I will give to mortals food, and not only drink.

[11694] Ampelos, your doom too is lovely; truly the threads

[11747] of Fate herself were softened for you and for your beauty,

[11807] for you even Hades was pitying, for you even Persephone

[11864] herself changed her harsh purpose,

[11900] and brought you, a corpse, to life for your brother Dionysus.

[11963] You did not die as Atymnius died; not the water of Styx,

[12021] not the flame of Tisiphone, you did not see the eye of Megaera;

[12086] you still live, boy, even though you died; nor did the water

[12148] of Lethe hide you, no common tomb holds you; but the earth

[12208] herself was ashamed to cover your form;

[12249] but my father made you a plant, honouring you as a son,

[12306] lord Cronion changed your body into sweet nectar.

[12357] Nature did not, as on the inscribed Therapnaean clusters,

[12416] carve a dirge upon your unweeping leaves;

[12459] you keep your own colour, boy, even among shoots;

[12510] your end has proclaimed the radiance of your limbs;

[12563] your reddening form has not yet abandoned you.

[12611] But avenging your death I will never cease

[12655] pouring your wine as a libation upon your slayer

[12705] as he is sacrificed, the man-slaying one. And you bring reproach to the Hamadryads

[12789] with your lovely leaves; and from your fragrant clusters

[12847] the moistures of your Loves breathe about me.

[12894] When shall I mingle the fruit of the apple in the bowl?

[12951] When shall I drop the fig into the nectar-cup?

[12999] The fig and the apple alike have grace as far as the teeth.

[13060] No other plant can contend with your grape-clusters;

[13114] not the rose, not the fair-coloured narcissus, not the anemone,

[13179] not the lily, not the hyacinth equals the shoot of Bacchus,

[13240] because with the new drops of much-pressed fruit

[13290] your drink will receive all the flowers; one drink will be

[13350] mingled with all, and will come to one fragrance,

[13401] blended with flowers of every kind; for your flower

[13454] will adorn the whole springtime meadow-grass.

[13501] Yield to me, famed-archer, because you girt your griefless locks

[13567] with a mournful bond of many-wailing leaves;

[13613] dirges are inscribed upon your petals; but if in a garden

[13672] the famed-archer bears a garland, I draw sweet wine,

[13726] and put about myself a lovely garland, and the sweet-drinking

[13789] Ampelos himself I lift wholly within my heart.

[13837] Yield to the grape-rich one, glittering-helmed; for bloody Ares

[13902] pours gore as a libation, while the vine-clad one pours to Dionysus

[13971] the reddening dew of the wine-drenched cluster.

[14020] Deo, you are plundered after Pallas; for the olives

[14073] bring forth no gladness, and no ear of grain charms a man,

[14133] the pear has honey-sweet fruit, the myrtle grows

[14183] fragrant flowers, but they do not, with a mind-charming fruit,

[14247] cast human cares to the winds;

[14279] I have become far better than you both; for when our

[14333] wine is not present, joyless are the meals of the table,

[14391] when wine is not present, uncharmed are the dances.

[14444] If you can, grey-eyed one, drink the fruit of your olive;

[14503] my splendid-gifted harvest has conquered your plant,

[14557] because with your sleek oil athlete-men, joyless,

[14608] anoint their bodies; but the sorely-suffering man,

[14660] having cast wife or daughter into a common doom,

[14710] or having grief for perished children or mother or father,

[14770] when he tastes sweet wine,

[14798] will shake off the gloomy, swelling mass of his grief.

[14854] Ampelos, even after death you gladden the heart of Bacchus;

[14915] with all my limbs I will mingle your drink."

[14961] And all about, the trees with downward-nodding head,

[15015] like a suppliant, bend the curving neck,

[15057] and the aged palm bowed down its high-stretched leaves;

[15114] about the apple-tree you stretch your feet, about the fig-tree

[15178] spreading your hands you lean upon them, and your

[15229] harvest the handmaids lighten, as a mistress,

[15276] when, with the coiling beat of your stretching leaves,

[15332] you rise above the shoulder of the attendants; and of the neighbouring plants

[15411] the soft, many-coloured leaves of the wide-scattered clusters,

[15475] as though you slept, quiver upon your face,

[15520] cooling breezes with sparing breaths,

[15559] as an attendant shakes a thin, accustomed fan,

[15607] bearing an artificial breeze, cool, for her king.

[15658] But if you bring the noonday threat of Phaethon,

[15708] the Etesian breeze, leading the way for your cluster, comes,

[15770] lulling to rest the thirsty star of fiery Maira,

[15820] when the course of the summer-born Hour heats you,

[15872] warming with Sirian vapour the dew as it ripens.

[15922] He spoke, exulting, and cast away his former cares,

[15975] holding the fragrant harvest, the medicine for the youth.

[16034] And these things are sung about the vine-clad cluster,

[16090] how it became named for the youth. But of the singers

[16145] there is another, older story, how once upon the earth

[16201] from heaven the fruit-bearing Olympian ichor flowed

[16254] and brought forth the drink of the Bacchic cluster, and on the crags

[16324] self-grown, untended, the fruit of the harvest grew;

[16378] and not yet was the vine named for the gardener, but in the thickets

[16448] the wild vine, flourishing among the much-curved parsley-plants,

[16514] put forth a vine-rich wood of wine-bearing plants,

[16566] gushing up the moist, crammed mass of its dew;

[16614] and there was a great row, where, creeping up in order,

[16671] cluster upon cluster, reddening, shook as it wandered;

[16727] of which one, half-finished, swelling its travail,

[16779] flushing in changing hues, appeared with two-coloured fruit;

[16841] another, whitening, ripened, of a colour like foam,

[16894] and a great one, golden-formed, pushed its yoked

[16944] neighbour, neighbour against neighbour, and another in form was like pitch,

[17021] darkening its whole flower, and from its wine-bearing leaves

[17083] it intoxicated the whole splendid-fruited olive growing near it;

[17149] and over another, just-carved, with unripe fruit,

[17200] the self-rushing black air ran upon the silvery cluster,

[17258] bearing, in its cluster-laden mass, the swelling harvest;

[17317] and the coiling tendril of the harvest crowned the opposite pine,

[17384] shading the enveloping shoot with combined thickets,

[17438] and delighted the heart of Pan; and bringing its branches,

[17498] shaken by Boreas, near the vine-clad cluster,

[17545] the blood-dipped pine waved its fragrant hair.

[17593] And about it a serpent, coiled with crooked spines,

[17646] milked the dainty nectar of the well-dropping fruit,

[17700] and milking the Bacchic drink with grim jaws,

[17747] dropping into its throat the draught of the wine-drenched cluster,

[17815] the serpent reddened its chin with a purple drop.

[17866] And the mountain-roaming god marvelled, watching the snake

[17926] with its throat stained with the wine-coloured drop;

[17980] and coiling its dappled, turning track of scales,

[18031] it entered the neighbouring deep-bosomed crevice in the rock,

[18094] the spangled snake, having beheld the Bacchic juice. And gazing,

[18160] Bacchus marked the cluster pregnant with the reddening dew,

[18221] and recalled the older oracles of oracular Rhea.

[18271] And he dug into the crags, and with the sharp point

[18324] of his earth-digging iron he hollowed a recess in the rock;

[18385] and smoothing the brows of the deepening hollows

[18435] he made the trench into the shape of a fine-grape wine-press,

[18498] reaping the fresh-budding clusters with a sharp thyrsus,

[18556] making the likeness of the later-born curved-clawed sickle.

[18617] And there was a band of Satyrs, his companions; of whom one

[18678] leaned, gathering the vintage, another received the clusters

[18740] cut, in a hollow vessel, another, cutting the tangled leaves,

[18803] shook off the green refuse of the wine-loving fruit;

[18857] another, without thyrsus and without well-sharpened iron,

[18916] stretching his ironless right hand to the branches,

[18969] broke off the tips of the coiling cluster,

[19013] crouching, bent over, stretching his eye toward the vine;

[19072] and in a deep hollow he spread the heaped harvest,

[19124] heaping the centre-naveled back of the ravine with grapes . . .

[19189] he laid the coiling clusters one upon another in the hollow,

[19251] stretched out this way and that, and as he heaped up vintage-piles

[19319] filling the whole hollow he gathered them in the cavernous rock,

[19385] and trod the cluster with the dancing beat of his feet.

[19442] And the Satyrs, shaking their wild hair into the air,

[19497] learning the like mimicry of Dionysus,

[19537] binding dappled fawnskins about their shoulders,

[19587] shouted the song of the Bacchic, fellow-tongued echo,

[19642] with much-leaping feet pressing the harvest,

[19688] singing "Euoi"; and from the grape-rich hollow

[19736] as the wine gushed up the ravines grew purple;

[19784] and the harvest, pressed by alternating feet,

[19831] cast up the white foam of the reddening dew.

[19877] And they drew with bovine horns instead of cups,

[19927] not yet appearing, whence afterward, ever from that time,

[19986] this wondrous name belonged to the wine being mingled.

[20042] And one, gushing up the moisture of mind-charming Bacchus,

[20102] bent his crooked track of feet with a coiling beat,

[20155] shifting his right sole, changing from the left,

[20205] and wetted his shaggy beard with the moisture of Bacchus;

[20264] another leapt up, shaken by the goad of drunkenness,

[20318] hearing the fearful bellow of the struck ox-hide;

[20369] and one, drinking the irresistible stream of pain-healing wine,

[20434] reddened his dark chin with the rosy draught;

[20481] another, stretching his unsteady gaze up toward a tree,

[20538] watched the half-revealed, unfilleted neighbour Nymph,

[20594] and now into the high-leaved plant of the mountain wood

[20651] he would have crept with the curved-clawed sole of his slipping foot,

[20722] had Dionysus not restrained him; and about the springs

[20778] another, with a wandering beat of mind-rousing drunkenness,

[20839] pursued the watery, unrobed Naiad girl,

[20880] and now he would have embraced her as she swam with his shaggy arm,

[20949] had she not, anticipating him, hidden in the deep stream.

[21008] But to the wine-drinker Dionysus alone Rhea gave

[21058] the amethyst, the warder-off of raving compulsion.

[21110] And many of the fair-horned Satyrs, with sport-loving sole,

[21171] goaded into the dance, reveled; of whom one,

[21217] holding a hot, new goad beneath his heart, the escort of the Loves,

[21286] clasped a Bacchante about the middle with his hairy arm;

[21344] another, shaken by the goad of mind-wandering drunkenness,

[21404] touched the girdle of an unmarried, chaste maiden,

[21456] dragging back to Cypris the garments of the unyielding nymph,

[21519] and with a back-cast hand touched her rosy thighs;

[21571] and one reined back an unwilling initiate girl

[21619] lighting the night-dancing torch kindled for Dionysus;

[21675] another, casting his sparing fingers about her breast,

[21731] squeezed the swelling circle of her unbending bosom.

[21785] And after the revel of his sweet harvest, Dionysus,

[21838] exulting, entered the caves of the Cybelean goddess,

[21892] stretching the cluster-laden branches with flower-loving hand,

[21956] and taught his own Maeonia his sleepless festival.
