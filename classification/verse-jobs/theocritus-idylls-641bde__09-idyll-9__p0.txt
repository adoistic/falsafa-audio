[0] 9. The Singing-Match: Daphnis and Menalcas Daphnis

[52] Sweetly the calf lows, and sweetly the cow,

[97] sweetly the pipe and the cowherd, and sweetly too I sing.

[156] I have a couch of leaves by the cool water, and on it are heaped

[222] the fair hides of white heifers, which all of them

[274] the southwest wind, as they browsed the arbutus, shook down from the crag.

[350] And of the burning summer I take as little heed

[399] as a lover takes of his father's words and his mother's.

[457] So Daphnis sang to me, and so did Menalcas.

[502] Menalcas

[512] Etna, my mother, I too dwell in a fair cave

[557] among the hollow rocks; and I have such things as appear

[615] in a dream: many sheep, and many she-goats,

[660] whose fleeces lie about my head and at my feet.

[709] In a fire of oak the puddings boil, and dry beech-nuts

[765] roast in the fire when it freezes; and I care no more

[820] for winter than a toothless man cares for nuts when porridge is by.

[889] I clapped my hands for them, and at once gave each a gift,

[949] to Daphnis a club, which my father's field reared for me,

[1008] self-grown, such as not even a craftsman could fault,

[1063] and to the other a fine spiral whelk-shell, whose flesh I myself

[1129] had eaten, watching among the Icarian rocks,

[1175] cutting it into five for the five of us; and he blew a blast on the shell.

[1251] Hail, pastoral Muses, and bring to light the songs

[1303] that once, in those herdsmen's company, I sang;

[1352] and may I never raise a blister on my tongue's tip again.

[1411] Cicada is dear to cicada, and ant to ant,

[1454] and hawks to hawks, but to me the Muse and song.

[1504] Of these may all my house be full. For neither sleep

[1558] nor sudden spring is sweeter, nor to the bees

[1605] their flowers; so dear to me are the Muses. Whomever they look upon

[1674] they make glad, and those Circe never harmed with her draught.
