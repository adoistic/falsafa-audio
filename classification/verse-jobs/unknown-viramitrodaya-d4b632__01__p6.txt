[667769] Furthermore, for one who has established the sacred fires, for the daughter's son, even when the maternal uncle, who is without fires, is present, for the sake of the *pārvaṇa* rite, the Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa of the maternal grandfather would be obtained, or for the sake of the daughter's son, the maternal uncle would have to perform it in a secondary time, and the separate verse, "not a *pārvaṇa*, not an auspicious rite," would be meaningless.

[668211] And the auspicious rite is one that must be performed, not an optional one.

[668288] For that can be performed separately even without the postponement of the Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa.

[668378] The time for the Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa performed by one without fires for a deceased who has established the sacred fires is stated by—

[668507] Laghu Hārīta:

[668522] > When the sacrificer is without fires, O hero, he should then perform it. / > If the deceased has established the sacred fires, his Sapiṇḍana is in the third fortnight. //

[668696] 'Sacrificer' (*gṛhī*) = the agent.

[668732] Sumantu also says:

[668752] > If the deceased has established the sacred fires, and the agent is without fires, / > the Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa should be performed then in the third fortnight. //

[668911] And here, in "his Sapiṇḍana is in the third fortnight," since the seventh case is heard, indicating the third fortnight as the locus, and it should not be argued that this is a *sati-saptamī* (locative absolute), and thus the Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa is on a *tithi* of the same kind as the death-*tithi*, as in the monthly and annual *śrāddhas*. Because by the maxim, "A case-ending indicating a grammatical role is stronger than one indicating a relationship," the locative of locus is stronger than the locative absolute, and by the force of the suffix indicating a completed number, only the second fortnight is obtained.

[669527] And it should not be argued that in the statement of Gobhila, "when the year is complete, in the sixth month, in the third fortnight, or on whichever day a *vṛddhi* occurs," just as 'complete' is connected with 'year', so too it is connected with 'sixth month' and 'third fortnight', and thus the day after that is to be taken.

[669856] Because for lack of authority, just as in "having sacrificed for a month with the *darśa-paurṇamāsa* sacrifices, they observe the *upasad* rites," the word 'complete' is not connected with '*upasad*', so too here, 'complete' is not connected with 'sixth month' and 'third fortnight'.

[670141] Therefore, the third fortnight, when it is complete, is the locus.

[670209] Hemādri, however, while saying that the three fortnights are complete, says that the day on which the third fortnight is completed is the locus, from the natural sense of the seventh case, not the day after it.

[670421] When the agent has established the sacred fires—

[670471] Sumantu says:

[670486] > If the sacrificer has established the sacred fires, O king, and the deceased is without fires, / > the Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa should be performed by the sons on the twelfth day. //

[670661] Kātyāyana says:

[670678] > Having completed the eleventh-day rite, before the new moon day, according to the rules, / > a Brahmin who has established the sacred fires should perform the Sapiṇḍana for his mother and father. //

[670880] Thus it is said.

[670898] Here, although it is said "before the new moon day," it should be performed only at the prescribed time stated by Baudhāyana, etc., "on the eleventh day or on the twelfth day," because when it can be justified without superseding that, supersession is not proper, and it would lead to the undesirable consequence of inferring an independent time.

[671246] {MV-S_223}

[671258] Hemādri, however, says that it is an independent, different time.

[671325] As for—

[671334] > Whichever new moon day is the tenth from the day of death, / > on it a son who has established the sacred fires should perform the Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa. //

[671486] —this verse of Jābāli, that refers to the tenth day from the day of death.

[671562] And thus, the eleventh day is stated.

[671601] And when the new moon day is after the twenty-second day, then having performed the sixteen *śrāddhas* in the twelve days after the impurity period, the Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa should be performed on the thirteenth day.

[671812] Concerning the Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa—

[671843] > Having performed the *śrāddha* like a monthly rite for twelve days, one should perform it on the thirteenth day.

[671959] This is from the statement of Viṣṇu.

[671997] The meaning of this is:

[672022] A *śrāddha* whose purpose is the same as that of the monthly rites, namely, the release from the state of a ghost.

[672138] The singular number is used with reference to the group.

[672196] There, the *ūnamāsika*, *traipakṣika*, and *ūnaṣāṇmāsika* should be performed on the first, second, sixth, and twelfth days, respectively, because the day is in place of the month.

[672378] For this very reason, it is said by him further on, "If there is an extra month within the year, one should increase one day for the monthly rite." And "twelve days" and "thirteen" are from the day after the impurity period, from the statement of Jāvāli, "after ten nights."

[672654] Here, the mention of ten nights is indicative of the respective impurity period.

[672736] Then, by one who has established the sacred fires, who is away from home, or whose fire has been interrupted, for the sake of the *pārvaṇa* *śrāddha*, the postponement should also be performed.

[672931] Thus—

[672938] In the *Hemādri*, Prajāpati says:

[672973] > A son who has established the sacred fires should not perform the *pitṛyajña* without having performed the Sapiṇḍana. / > Performing a *pārvaṇa* or an auspicious rite, he does not obtain the fruit. //

[673177] Because it is mentioned together with the auspicious rite, which must be performed, and because in "before the new moon day," from the mention of the new moon day, the *pitṛyajña* related to the new moon day, like the *pārvaṇa*, is also taken.

[673422] And this, in the view of those for whom the postponement of the Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa is for the sake of the *Piṇḍapitṛyajña*, should be performed for the mother as well as for the father.

[673604] > She attains oneness with her husband in the *piṇḍa*, the *gotra*, and the impurity. / > The wife of the husband's ancestors, therefore, she is a sharer in that *gotra*. // > After the Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa, whatever is given to the ancestors, / > in all that, she who has gone becomes a sharer; this is the firm decision in the *dharmas*. //

[673941] From these verses of Yajñapārśva and Baudhāyana, because she has a share in the father's *piṇḍa*.

[674040] And it should not be argued that in that case, she would become the recipient.

[674120] > She attains one form when the Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa has been performed.

[674187] From this verse, even if she is not the recipient as being with her husband, her enjoyment of a share of the husband's *piṇḍa* is stated.

[674326] Otherwise, in the Sapiṇḍana with the maternal grandfather, she would not be a partaker of the maternal grandfather's *piṇḍa*.

[674453] For the maternal grandfathers are not the recipients as being with their daughters, for lack of authority.

[674573] Therefore, by an agent who has established the sacred fires, after the impurity period, having performed the monthly rites in twelve days, on the thirteenth day, or from the day of death, on the twelfth day, or on the eleventh day itself, if the new moon day falls there, the Sapiṇḍana should be performed there.

[674887] And here, if it is not performed due to carelessness, it should be performed at the prescribed times in the third fortnight, etc.

[675018] Thus—

[675025] Gobhila says:

[675040] > The Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa, if not performed at the times of the twelve days, etc., due to carelessness, / > one should perform at the succeeding times. //

[675190] And this applies only to one who has established the sacred fires.

[675258] For him alone, the twelve days, etc., are the primary times, and the succeeding ones are enjoined as secondary options, but not for one without fires.

[675410] For him, the end of the year is the primary time, and no time is prescribed after that.

[675499] In the *Bhaviṣya Purāṇa*:

[675526] > On the twelfth day, or in the sixth month, or in the third fortnight, or in the third month, / > or in the eleventh month, or when an auspicious rite is imminent. //

[675695] Here, in "in the sixth," 'month' is connected.

[675743] Thus—

[675750] Baudhāyana says: "Now, when the year is complete, the Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa is in the third fortnight, in the third month, in the sixth, or in the eleventh, or on the twelfth day."

[675924] Here too, by the previously stated reasoning, since the locus is established, the explanation of some, "in the sixth month that has passed," the source for that should be sought.

[676104] He states another time—

[676129] Paiṭhīnasi says: "At the end of a year is the dismissal; in the ninth month, say some."

[676218] Viṣṇu also says:

[676236] > Having performed the *śrāddha* like a monthly rite for twelve days, one should perform it on the thirteenth day.

[676352] Vyāghra has stated the excellence of the twelfth day in general:

[676418] > Due to the infinity of family duties, and the shortness of men's lives, / > and the instability of the body, the twelfth day is praised. //

[676561] 'Of family duties' = of the performance of tonsure, etc., which have a fixed time according to family custom and which have a *vṛddhi* *śrāddha*.

[676708] 'Instability' is the possibility of having to leave the country due to royal oppression, etc., or the incapacity for rites due to disease, etc.

[676853] 'Excellence' is that by this, performed at that time, just as by what is performed at the primary time, the release from the state of a ghost occurs.

[677004] 'Twelfth day' (*dvādaśāha*), by the derivation "the twelfth day," is the twelfth day, and that is from the day of death.

[677126] The 'twelve-day period' (*dvādaśāha*) is a collection of twelve days, the time for the postponement of the sixteen *śrāddhas*, and that is in the form of an auspicious occasion, says Śūlapāṇi.

[677320] But surely, how can this be praised? The Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa is for the purpose of attaining the state of a Father, and what is performed on the twelfth day alone does not bring about that state of a Father.

[677523] Because—

[677533] In the *Viṣṇudharmottara*:

[677561] > When the Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa has been performed, a man, after a year, /

[677642] > having abandoned the body of a ghost, obtains a body for enjoyment. //

[677716] Thus it is said.

[677734] In the *Agni Purāṇa* also:

[677762] > For whom the Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa is before a year, / > his state of a ghost is to be known for a year, O king! //

[677873] Thus it is said.

[677891] Therefore, even if the Sapiṇḍana is performed before, since the release from the state of a ghost is at the end of a year, how can this be praised? If this is so, these two verses mean that if the Sapiṇḍana is performed without having performed the sixteen *śrāddhas*, the state of a Father is not attained.

[678200] > Without having given the sixteen *śrāddhas*, one should not perform the Sapiṇḍana.

[678286] Because it forms a single sentence with this.

[678333] From this very verse, it is also said that the sixteen *śrāddhas* are to be postponed.

[678421] How else, if the sixteen *śrāddhas* are not postponed, could the Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa *śrāddha*, which is performed without the release from the state of a ghost that depends on them, be meaningful?

[678614] But surely, if the Sapiṇḍana is performed after postponing the sixteen *śrāddhas*, since the release from the state of a ghost has occurred, the monthly and water-pot *śrāddhas* would not occur again.

[678816] True, if—

[678827] > For whom the Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa is before a year, / > the monthly rite and the water-pot should be given for him for a year. //

[678953] —this verse of Laugākṣi did not exist.

[678993] Therefore, the repetition is from the verse alone.

[679045] Some, however, say that in a Sapiṇḍana postponed without the occasion of a *vṛddhi*, the release from the state of a ghost is at the end of a year.

[679194] In the postponement for the sake of a *vṛddhi*, however, the release from the state of a ghost is immediately after the Sapiṇḍana.

[679326] For this very reason, having stated the postponement of the Sapiṇḍana with "before a year, in a *vṛddhi*," he says—

[679443] Śātātapa says:

[679459] > There is no separate offering of the *piṇḍa* after that, / > for all the deceased here, who are joined by the mantras. // > And from the state of a ghost, they are freed here, and have reached the host of the Fathers. //

[679683] Thus it is said.

[679701] Or, if the performance is in the secondary time of Ekādaśī, and by chance a *vṛddhi*, etc., occurs after that, then having reached the time of the *vṛddhi*, the release from the state of a ghost occurs, because that is equal to the time at the end of the year, they say.

[679973] Here, this appears:

[679994] How is this equal to the time at the end of the year?

[680049] If it is said that because it is enjoined here as a Sapiṇḍana that has the release from the state of a ghost as its fruit, like the twelfth day for one who has established the sacred fires, as being primary, then it must be said that the secondary one also has been enjoined.

[680326] And the primary time is not the cause in itself; otherwise, even in the absence of a Sapiṇḍana at the end of a year, the release from the state of a ghost would occur. Therefore, it must be said that only a Sapiṇḍana performed at the primary time is the cause of the release from the state of a ghost.

[680629] This being so, since what is enjoined cannot be meaningless, let it be that what is performed at the enjoined time is the cause of the release from the state of a ghost. What need is there for its being primary?

[680842] {MV-S_226}

[680854] Therefore, only the Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa accompanied by the sixteen *śrāddhas* is the cause of the release from the state of a ghost, not that which is accompanied by the primary time.

[681033] Some, however, say that all times are primary with respect to one who has established the sacred fires, and thus a Sapiṇḍana performed on the twelfth, etc., days, like one performed before a *vṛddhi*, is the cause of the release from the state of a ghost.

[681290] And those times are:

[681312] There, in the *Caturviṃśatimata*:

[681347] > The Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa is when the year is complete, or on an auspicious occasion, / > on the twelfth day, in the view of some, and on the eleventh likewise. //

[681506] Thus it is said.

[681524] Some, however, say that this verse, which collects all the primary times, is not for the purpose of stating the eleventh day, etc., for one without fires.

[681680] Otherwise, it would also establish the time at the end of the year for one with fires.

[681768] And thus, with respect to each, it enjoins only those times that are obtained from verses such as "Whichever new moon day is the first," and does not have a separate *śruti* as its source, they say.

[681968] In the *Bhaviṣya* in the *Parāśaramādhavīya*:

[682015] > A sacrificer who is without fires should perform the Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa / > for a deceased who is without the sacred fires, when the year is complete, O bull among the Bharatas! // > On the twelfth day, or in the sixth month, or in the third fortnight, or in the third month, / > or in the eleventh month, or when an auspicious rite is imminent. //

[682362] Thus it is said.

[682380] And all these are primary.

[682408] Of these, the twelfth day is praised.

[682447] From the verse, "Due to the infinity," etc.

[682492] For the others, there is an option by choice, says Mādhava.

[682553] Others, however, say that the end of the year is primary; the eleventh month, which is proximate to it, is secondary.

[682672] Likewise, the others, which are more remote, are secondary in the reverse order.

[682754] And when the sixteen *śrāddhas* are postponed for the sake of the Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa, they should be performed on a *tithi* of the same kind as the death-*tithi*.

[682913] Because at least one primary time is obtained.

[682961] If that is not possible, then on the day of the Sapiṇḍana itself, because of the proximity of the occasion.

[683070] Or, when there is a gap in time, from the previously cited verse of Viṣṇu, one should perform the sixteen *śrāddhas* in twelve days.

[683204] For Śūdras, however, the Sapiṇḍana is on the twelfth day.

[683263] Thus—

[683270] Viṣṇu says: "For Śūdras, it is on the twelfth day, without mantras."

[683340] And it should not be argued that since Kātyāyana has enjoined, "For all the social classes, the Sapiṇḍana is at the end of the impurity period," and since a Śūdra has an impurity period of a month, and since the tenth of the body-completing *piṇḍas* is enjoined for him at the end of the impurity period, how can the Sapiṇḍana for a Śūdra be on the twelfth day?

[683703] Because, by taking the view, "For all the social classes, the impurity is for ten days," or by accepting the cessation of the impurity period by the force of the verse, as in the injunction of the eleventh day for the *darśa* *śrāddha* of a good Śūdra, or by the force of the verse, it should be performed even in the middle of the impurity period, there is no fault.

[684072] {MV-S_227}

[684084] In reality, the term 'twelfth day' does not refer to the twelfth day from the day of death, but rather, after the end of the impurity period, having performed the twelve monthly rites in twelve days like a twice-born, the Sapiṇḍana should be performed on the twelfth day, not on the thirteenth day like a twice-born.

[684402] For this very reason, it is said there, "If there is an extra month, then on the thirteenth day."

[684501] By this, the view that there is an increase of some *śrāddha*, not an increase of a day, is also refuted.

[684608] For a son who is in another country, however, there is a special rule.

[684680] There, Śātātapa says:

[684703] > For sons who have gone to another country, how should it be? / > Having heard the news, the shaving of the head should be done, and for ten days, the offering of sesame-water. // > Then one should perform the Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa on the eleventh day.

[684950] When the time at the end of the year has passed, another time is stated in the *Mādhavīya*—

[685043] Ṛṣyaśṛṅga says:

[685060] > If the Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa *śrāddha* is not performed at the stated time, / > one should perform it in Raudra, Hasta, Rohiṇī, or Maitra. //

[685197] And this should be seen as being when, due to the imminence of a *vṛddhi*, etc., another prescribed time is not available, in accordance with the *nakṣatra*, etc.

[685361] Thus ends the time for the Sapiṇḍana.

[685400] Now, the determination of the *tithi* for a general *śrāddha*.

[685464] A *śrāddha* is of three kinds: *pārvaṇa*, *ekoddiṣṭa*, and *daivika*.

[685535] There, Kaṇva says:

[685555] > The *śrāddha* that is for one recipient is proclaimed as *ekoddiṣṭa*. / > That which is for three recipients, that the sages know as *pārvaṇa*. //

[685705] Pāraskara says:

[685722] > What is performed with the gods as the recipients, that is called *daivika*. / > One should perform that like an obligatory *śrāddha*, on Dvādaśī, etc., with effort. //

[685894] There, if the *tithi* on which a *pārvaṇa* *śrāddha* is enjoined falls on two days, then on whichever day, the preceding or the succeeding, it pervades the afternoon, on that day the *pārvaṇa* should be performed.

[686109] > Whichever rite has whichever time, the *tithi* pervading that time / > should be used for those rites; decrease and increase are not the cause. //

[686259] This is from the verse of Yājñavalkya.

[686299] > Whichever *tithi* pervades midday, that would be the *tithi* for an *ekoddiṣṭa*. / > Whichever pervades the afternoon, that would be the *tithi* for a *pārvaṇa*. //

[686467] And from the verse of Gautama.

[686499] When it pervades the afternoon on the preceding day, then the preceding one should be taken.

[686593] {MV-S_228}

[686605] Thus says Manu:

[686622] > The *tithi* on which the sun sets, that the ancestors attend. / > For that was given to the ancestors by the Self-born one, the afternoon. //

[686767] The meaning is that the *tithi* which is connected with the afternoon and on which the sun sets, that is praised for rites for the ancestors.

[686910] Thus says Hārīta:

[686929] > The afternoon belongs to the ancestors; the *tithi* that follows the afternoon / > should be taken for a rite for the ancestors, not the one that follows the setting. //

[687102] 'Follows the setting' = follows only the setting.

[687153] And thus, the meaning is that the *tithi* which pervades the afternoon and follows the setting should be taken for a rite for the ancestors.

[687295] When, however, it does not pervade the afternoon on either of the two days, then the preceding one should be taken.

[687412] Thus—

[687419] Vṛddha Yājñavalkya says:

[687445] > For a rite for the gods, the *tithi* on which the sun has risen should be known. / > For a rite for the ancestors, the *tithi* on which the sun has set should be known. //

[687620] In the *Śivarahasya* and *Saurapurāṇa*:

[687661] > For the most part, the end of the *tithi* should be fasted on by those who desire the fruit of a divine rite. / > The beginning is for the satisfaction of the ancestors; it is said by the great sages to be for the ancestors. //

[687892] In the *Māradoyapurāṇa*:

[687918] > The end of the *tithi* is called divine; the wise know it is to be fasted on. / > The beginning of the *tithi* is proclaimed as ancestral by the knowers of the scriptures and the knowers of time. // > In an ancestral rite, if it is touched at the time of sunset, it is proclaimed as complete. / > The one at sunrise should not be taken there; in a divine rite, the *tithi* is the one at sunrise. //

[688320] Gobhila says:

[688335] > The *tithi* that pervades the evening is cited for a *pārvaṇa* rite.

[688407] And the pervasion of the evening is only for one that is not less than three *muhūrtas*.

[688497] Baudhāyana also says:

[688520] > For a divine rite, when the sun has risen; for an ancestral rite, when the sun is setting. / > For two *muhūrtas* or three of the day, that *tithi* is for the *havya* and *kavya*. //

[688706] When the sun has risen, the two *muhūrtas* are for a divine rite, that is, fit for a divine rite.

[688805] When the sun has begun to set, the three *muhūrtas* before sunset are for an ancestral rite, that is, fit for an ancestral rite.

[688935] Therefore, the *tithi* present in that time should be taken for the *havya* and *kavya*. This is the meaning.

[689046] When it pervades a part of the afternoon on two days unequally, then the one with the greater pervasion should be taken, by the maxim, "Even a small difference is a deciding factor."

[689230] {MV-S_229}

[689242] When it pervades a part of the afternoon on two days equally, that occurs by the increase, decrease, or equality of the *tithi* of the *śrāddha*. There, on which day the *pārvaṇa* should be performed, in that doubt—

[689459] > A short one, a proud one, and a violent one—this is the threefold nature of a *tithi*. / > The all-proud ones are to be worshipped on the succeeding day; the violent one would be on the preceding day. //

[689666] —the determination is from this verse of Vyāghra.

[689717] `Kharva` is a *tithi* of equal duration; `darpa` is increase, a *tithi* with that; `hiṃsā` is decrease, a *tithi* with that. This is the threefold nature, the form, of a *tithi*.

[689897] Here, since it is stated that the `sarva` (all) and `darpa` (proud) are to be taken on the succeeding day, and the `hiṃsā` (violent) on the preceding day, the qualities of `sarvatva` (all-ness), etc., belong to the *tithi* to be taken.

[690134] And that equal partial pervasion occurs by the increase, decrease, and equality of the *tithi* to be taken, as follows: In a day-and-night of equal duration, when the preceding *tithi* is twenty *ghaṭikās* and the succeeding *tithi* is twenty-two *ghaṭikās*, then on both days there is an equal partial pervasion of the afternoon of four *ghaṭikās*.

[690485] And the *tithi* to be taken increases by two *ghaṭikās*.

[690543] And when the preceding *tithi* is twenty-two *ghaṭikās* and the succeeding *tithi* is twenty *ghaṭikās*, then there is an equal partial pervasion of the afternoon of two *ghaṭikās*, and the *tithi* decreases by two *ghaṭikās*.

[690771] And when on the preceding and succeeding days, the preceding and succeeding *tithis* are twenty-one *ghaṭikās* each, then there is an equal partial pervasion of the afternoon of three *ghaṭikās*, and the *tithi* is of equal duration.

[691006] Here, in cases of increase and equality, the succeeding day should be taken; in a case of decrease, the preceding.

[691122] Thus ends the determination of the *pārvaṇa* rite.

[691174] ---

[691179] ### [Determination of the Ekoddiṣṭa Śrāddha Time]

[691230] Hārīta states the time for an *ekoddiṣṭa*:

[691274] > An *āmaśrāddha* is in the forenoon; an *ekoddiṣṭa*, at midday. / > A *pārvaṇa* is in the afternoon; and the one occasioned by a *vṛddhi*, in the morning. //

[691434] Vyāsa states the time of commencement:

[691474] > In the first part of the *kutapa*, one should begin an *ekoddiṣṭa*. / > Or near the turning-point, there itself, with a restrained soul. //

[691617] Ślokagautama states the time of completion:

[691662] > Having begun a *śrāddha* in the *kutapa*, a wise man should perform it until the *rauhiṇa*. / > A knower of the rules, following the rules, should not transgress the *rauhiṇa*. //

[691845] And thus, since the *kutapa* and *rauhiṇa* *muhūrtas* are the time for an *ekoddiṣṭa*, the determination is by their pervasion, etc.

[691979] Here too, in the two cases where there is a pervasion of midday on the preceding day only, or on the succeeding day only—

[692102] > Whichever *tithi* pervades midday, whether the preceding or the succeeding, / > on it one should give to the ancestors; decrease and increase are not the cause. //

[692269] {MV-S_230}

[692281] —the determination is from this verse of Baudhāyana.

[692335] When it pervades midday on both days, or does not touch it, the performance is on the preceding day.

[692437] > For a rite for the gods, the *tithi* on which the sun has risen should be known. / > For a rite for the ancestors, the *tithi* on which the sun has set should be known. //

[692612] This is from the verse of Vṛddha Yājñavalkya and the previously cited verses of the *Śivarahasya*, *Saurapurāṇa*, etc.

[692732] When there is an equal partial pervasion on both days, the determination is from the *kharva*, etc., verse, as before.

[692852] When there is an unequal partial pervasion, the determination is by the greater pervasion.

[692944] The time for a *daivika* *śrāddha* is stated—

[692991] In the *Brahma Purāṇa*:

[693016] > A *daivika* *śrāddha* is in the forenoon; a *pārvaṇa*, in the afternoon. / > An *ekoddiṣṭa* is at midday; the one occasioned by a *vṛddhi*, in the morning. //

[693178] The forenoon is—

[693196] > The forenoon is before the turning-point; the afternoon is after it.

[693268] —thus stated.

[693283] There too—

[693295] > In the morning, a best of the twice-born should not perform a *śrāddha*. / > In occasional *śrāddhas*, a regulation of time is not remembered. //

[693444] From this verse cited by Mādhava in the *Śivarāghavasaṃvāda*, since the morning is prohibited in *śrāddhas* other than the *vṛddhi* *śrāddha* which is enjoined for the morning, the time for a *daivika* *śrāddha* is the forenoon other than that, and the determination is by its pervasion, as before.

[693744] A special rule is that when, by increase, decrease, etc., there is an equal pervasion of the time of the rite on both days, the succeeding day should be taken.

[693905] > For a divine rite, when the sun has risen; for an ancestral rite, when the sun is setting. / > For two *muhūrtas* or three of the day, that *tithi* is for the *havya* and *kavya*. //

[694091] This is from the verse of Baudhāyana.

[694130] The *kharva*, etc., verse applies to an ancestral rite other than this.

[694203] Thus ends the determination of the *tithi* for a general *śrāddha*.

[694277] ### [The Divisions of the Day and the Definition of Aparāhṇa]

[694340] Now, the definitions of *aparāhṇa*, etc.

[694382] In the *Skanda Purāṇa*:

[694407] > The forenoon is before the turning-point; the afternoon is after it.

[694479] Thus it is said.

[694497] Here, in "before the turning-point" (*āvartanāttu pūrvāhṇaḥ*), the `āṅ` prefix should be joined.

[694595] The turning-point is midday. And so, the part before the turning-point is the forenoon; the part after that is the afternoon. This is the meaning.

[694743] With this very division in mind—

[694777] Manu says (a. 3, ślo. 278):

[694806] > And just as the latter fortnight is superior to the former fortnight, / > so the afternoon of a *śrāddha* is superior to the forenoon. //

[694947] Thus it is said.

[694965] Here, since the former and latter fortnights, which are equal parts of a month, are used as an example, it is understood that the forenoon and afternoon are the former and latter halves of the day.

[695164] {MV-S_231}

[695176] The three parts of a day divided into three—forenoon, midday, and afternoon—are employed in the *śruti*:

[695282] > The forenoon belongs to the gods, midday to men, and the afternoon to the ancestors; therefore, one gives in the afternoon.

[695409] Here, for the sake of praising the afternoon, which is to be enjoined, the connection of the forenoon and midday with the gods and men, which is stated but not known from any other authority, is used to infer the injunctions, "one gives to the gods in the forenoon, and to men at midday."

[695699] In the *Skanda Purāṇa*:

[695724] > The five *muhūrtas* after sunrise are proclaimed as the first, the forenoon. / > Midday is after that; the afternoon is after that, five *muhūrtas*. //

[695879] Śātātapa also says:

[695900] > Therefore, in the forenoon of the day, the gods partake of the food. / > At midday, men; in the afternoon, the ancestors. //

[696028] Gobhila states a fourfold division:

[696065] > The forenoon is a *prahara* and a half; midday is a *prahara* likewise. / > The afternoon is from the third; and the evening is after that. //

[696211] Thus it is said.

[696229] The *Śatapatha śruti* indicates a fivefold division: "The sun is indeed all the seasons. When it rises, it is spring; when it is the time of the *saṅgava*, it is summer; when it is midday, it is the rainy season; when it is the afternoon, it is autumn; when it sets, it is winter."

[696512] Of a day consisting of fifteen *muhūrtas*, one part is three *muhūrtas*.

[696586] Thus, there are five parts, named morning (*prātaḥ*), late morning (*saṅgava*), midday (*madhyāhna*), afternoon (*aparāhṇa*), and evening (*sāyāhna*).

[696738] Vyāsa also says:

[696756] > Three *muhūrtas* are the morning; the late morning is just as long. / > Midday is three *muhūrtas*; the afternoon is also of that length. // > The evening is three *muhūrtas*, excluded from all *dharmic* rites.

[696970] Thus it is said.

[696988] A fifteen-fold division is stated in the *Prabhāsakhaṇḍa*:

[697048] > The *muhūrtas* of the day should be known as fifteen, always.

[697113] Thus it is said.

[697131] Whether the day is thirty *ghaṭikās* or less or more, the *muhūrtas* are fifteen only; this is the meaning of 'always'.

[697252] The names of the *muhūrtas* in order are:

[697295] > Raudra, Śveta, and Maitra, and likewise Sārabhaṭa is remembered. / > Sāvitra, Vaiśvadeva, Gāndharva, and Kutapa likewise. // > Rohiṇa, Tilaka, Vibhava, and Nikṛti. /

[697464] {MV-S_232}

[697476] > Śambara and Vijaya are remembered as the fifteen divisions. //

[697542] Thus it is said.

[697560] The eightfold, etc., divisions are not described, because there is no such practice.

[697646] Now, the measures of these parts of the day are determined.

[697707] There, the morning and late morning are of three *muhūrtas* each. The forenoon, in a twofold division, is seven and a half *muhūrtas*; in a threefold division, five *muhūrtas*; in a fourfold division, a *prahara* and a half.

[697933] Thus, the forenoon is of three kinds.

[697972] Midday also, in a threefold division, is five *muhūrtas*; in a fourfold division, a *prahara*; in a fivefold division, three *muhūrtas*. Thus, it is of three kinds.

[698138] The afternoon also, in a twofold division, is seven and a half *muhūrtas*; in a threefold division, five *muhūrtas*; in a fourfold division, half a *prahara*; in a fivefold division, three *muhūrtas*. Thus, the afternoon is of four kinds.

[698378] The evening, in a fourfold division, is a *prahara*; in a fivefold division, three *muhūrtas*. Thus, the evening is of two kinds.

[698509] And these forenoons, etc., are to be taken in their respective rites as enjoined and as available, like rice and barley.

[698631] Or, from verses such as "The forenoon is before the turning-point," since a time of seven and a half *muhūrtas* is understood as the forenoon, and since a shorter time of a *prahara* and a half, etc., which is included in it, is also obtained as being the forenoon because it is included in it, and since verses such as "The forenoon is a *prahara* and a half" would be meaningless, a gradation of forenoons, etc., of greater and lesser measure should be inferred, says Hemādri.

[699111] Now, the *kutapa* time.

[699136] In the *Skanda* and *Vāyu Purāṇas*:

[699173] > When the lord of rays (the sun) with his rays heats the earth (*ku*) completely for a moment, / > that time is to be known as *kutapa*; what is given there has great fruit. //

[699352] *Kuṃ* = the earth.

[699372] *Gopatiḥ* = the sun.

[699394] *Gobhiḥ* = with his rays.

[699421] The state of heating the entire earth belongs to the sun when it is at the zenith; the time indicated by that is called *kutapa*. This is the meaning.

[699573] And that is the eighth *muhūrta* of the day.

[699619] Thus it is said—

[699637] In the *Vāyu Purāṇa*:

[699660] > After the seventh *muhūrta* and before the ninth *muhūrta*, / > that time is to be known as *kutapa*; what is given to the ancestors is imperishable. //

[699816] In the *Kālikā Purāṇa*:

[699841] > A Brahmin, a blanket, cows, silver, fire, and guests, / > sesame seeds, Darbha grass, and midday—these nine are remembered as *ku-tāpas*. //

[699985] {MV-S_233}

[699997] In the *Prabhāsakhaṇḍa* also:

[700028] > Midday, a rhinoceros-horn vessel, and likewise a Nepalese blanket, / > silver, Darbha grass, sesame seeds, cows, and a daughter's son is remembered as the eighth. // > Sin is called *ku* (base), and these are its destroyers (*tāpa*). / > Because these eight are so, they are renowned as *kutapas*. //

[700332] Thus ends the *kutapa*.

[700362] ### [The Jurisprudence of Prohibitions (Paryudāsa vs. Pratiṣedha)]

[700430] Now, the time for *śrāddha*.

[700460] There, first, the prohibitions are described.

[700507] Manu says (a. 3, ślo. 280):

[700536] > One should not perform a *śrāddha* at night, for it is proclaimed as demonic. / > And in both twilights, and when the sun has not yet risen. //

[700683] Thus it is said.

[700701] Here, by 'twilight', it is not the twilight defined as:

[700758] > The twilight before sunrise is desired to be three *ghaṭikās*. / > The evening twilight is three *ghaṭikās* after the sun has set. //

[700895] that is taken.

[700911] Rather, it is the primary one, in the form of the time when the sun's disc is visible.

[700999] Because the defined one is already established by the prohibition of the night.

[701080] In the *Matsya Purāṇa*:

[701105] > The evening is three *muhūrtas*; a *śrāddha* should not be performed there.

[701184] Baudhāyana says:

[701202] > When the fourth *prahara* has arrived, a *śrāddha* should not be performed there.

[701287] And these are not simple prohibitions (*pratiṣedha*), because it would lead to the undesirable consequence of an option (*vikalpa*).

[701421] And the avoidance of an option is not as in "one who is initiated does not give."

[701504] Because there, both the injunction and the prohibition, being in the same context, are for the sake of the *śrāddha*, and thus have different scopes.

[701655] Therefore, these are only prohibitive exceptions (*paryudāsa*).

[701720] As for what is said here by Śūlapāṇi and others, that the acceptance of a *paryudāsa* is not out of fear of a *vikalpa*, because since the injunction and the prohibition are in the form of a general and a specific rule, they are not of equal strength, and thus a *vikalpa* does not arise, but rather for the sake of the simplicity of a single sentence, that is based on ignorance of the source texts.

[702122] Otherwise, even in "he does not use those two for the animal," there would be the undesirable consequence of the absence of a *vikalpa*.

[702260] Therefore, it is a *paryudāsa* by the stated method.

[702314] And the nature of these as a *paryudāsa* is for the sake of being a subordinate element of the injunction of the new moon day, in the sense of "on a new moon day other than at night, etc.," says Śūlapāṇi.

[702520] That is incorrect.

[702540] Because the parts of the night, etc., are parts of a civil day, and are not parts of the lunar *tithi* called Amāvāsyā, such a *paryudāsa* is not possible.

[702697] Therefore, the *paryudāsa* is only by the restriction of the civil day, in the sense of "in a part of the civil day other than the night, etc."

[702842] And it should not be argued that since there is no injunction of a civil day, how can it be a *paryudāsa* as a subordinate element of that?

[702983] Because the injunctions for Amāvāsyā, etc., themselves enjoin the civil day.

[703061] For they, due to the impossibility of performing a rite without a day-and-night period, refer to a day-and-night period that possesses it.

[703201] {MV-S_234}

[703213] Just as the hearing of spring, etc., as an occasion, because it is impossible for one who is not alive to perform it, refers to one who is alive and delimited by spring, etc.

[703389] For this very reason, in the resolve-statement 'Today...', etc., the learned mention the civil day as the locus.

[703503] And in the *Purāṇas* also, the resolve-statement "Today, having reached Janmāṣṭamī..." indicates an injunction of the civil day.

[703633] Therefore, the *paryudāsa* is only by the restriction of the civil day.

[703706] But surely, since even a *paryudāsa* requires the possibility of what is to be excluded, and since in all *śrāddhas*, the respective times of forenoon, afternoon, etc., are fixed, and there is no possibility of performing them at night, etc., how can these be *paryudāsas*? If this is so,

[703996] the *paryudāsa* of the morning twilight and its proximity applies to the *vṛddhi śrāddha* other than the one for the birth of a son, which is obtained from the verse occasioned by the increase of the *tithi* in the morning.

[704221] And the proximity here is also a single *muhūrta*, from the statement of Śātātapa, "and in the *muhūrta* of sunrise."

[704340] The *paryudāsas* of the evening twilight, its proximity, the evening, etc., apply to the *pārvaṇa śrāddha*.

[704449] Because by verses such as, "For a *śrāddha*, the afternoon is superior to the forenoon," and "in the third part of the day," the afternoon, which is included in the twofold and threefold divisions and extends to the night, is enjoined there.

[704692] And the fourth *prahara*, the evening, the eighth part of the day, and the last *muhūrta* should be seen as to be avoided in a graded manner.

[704835] The *paryudāsa* of the night, however, applies to the *śrāddha* occasioned by an eclipse, the birth of a son, etc.

[704951] Because for them, by verses of Devala, etc., such as, "At the sight of Rāhu, a *saṅkrānti*, a marriage, an emergency, and a *vṛddhi* rite, they should perform bathing, giving, etc., at night, and in optional vows," it is obtained at night.

[705192] Some say that even for the prohibition in the context of the new moon, since its inclusion in the context is impossible, there is an *utkarṣa* (transference).

[705352] Others, however, say that since the injunction and the *paryudāsa* have the same object, such as an eclipse, and are thus of equal strength, and it would lead to the flaw of an option, the *paryudāsa* of the night applies to a *tīrtha śrāddha*.

[705598] For that, since the afternoon obtained from the original model is superseded by the immediacy of the occasion enjoined by direct statement, is obtained generally even at night, but there is no injunction with that as its basis, as there is for an eclipse, etc., and thus there is no possibility of an option there, that alone is the object of the *paryudāsa* of the night.

[705972] As for the verse, "At an improper time or at a proper time, a *tīrtha śrāddha* and *tarpaṇa*," that too, because it is connected with the word 'or' (*vā*), is for the purpose of indicating the primacy of the secondary times of morning, evening, etc., which are obtained elsewhere than the night, which is excluded by the *paryudāsa* of another verse, and is not for the purpose of stating that it should be performed even at night, they say.

[706415] Others, however, say that when it is possible without superseding the context, its supersession is improper, and so, in the original rite itself, if, due to a divine or human obstacle, the *śrāddha* is not completed, and the sun should set, then because it has been commenced, and its completion at night is possible, the *paryudāsa* is "one should not perform a *śrāddha* at night."

[706800] And so, having left it half-done, having performed it on the next day, it should be completed, and the sacrificer also should eat only after that.

[706948] {MV-S_235}

[706960] For this very reason, the sūtra of Āpastamba, "Or in a commenced rite, the connection is until the completion," is in accordance with the commentary of the student, they say.

[707136] In reality, however, the injunctions of the afternoon, etc., are not for the purpose of a rule, but for the purpose of a great excellence.

[707276] Because from the verses of Śātātapa, etc., "The *śrāddha* of the new moon day that is proclaimed, that is known as *pārvaṇa*. In the afternoon, for the ancestors, the giving there is praised," it is understood to be so.

[707497] And so, since they are not fixed, and since all times are possible there, the *paryudāsa* of the night is proper.

[707612] Therefore, it is established that the time for non-occasional *śrāddhas* is any of the *muhūrtas* that fall between the night, the two twilights, and the morning and evening that are proximate to them, other than those.

[707833] Among them also, the five *muhūrtas* beginning with the *kutapa* are most praised in a *pārvaṇa* rite.

[707937] From the statement of Śātātapa: "The four *muhūrtas* after the *kutapa* *muhūrta*, or the five *muhūrtas*, are desired as the abode of the *svadhā*."

[708088] And in an *ekoddiṣṭa*, midday is praised.

[708131] And that here, having excluded the Gāndharva, is only the two *muhūrtas* of the *kutapa* and the *rohiṇa*.

[708239] From the statement of Gautama: "Having begun a *śrāddha* in the *kutapa*, a wise man should perform it until the *rauhiṇa*. A knower of the rules, following the rules, should not transgress the *rauhiṇa*."

[708446] And for the evening, even though it is prohibited, because it is enjoined again by the statement of Vyāsa, "If one's own time has passed, one should perform it before the night; that is its limit," it is secondary.

[708662] There too, the first, second, and third *muhūrtas* are secondary, more secondary, and most secondary.

[708765] Because the eighth part of the day and the last *muhūrta* are prohibited again, it is not enjoined in all cases, like the night and morning.

[708907] Because for them, there is no re-enjoinment in the case of a *pārvaṇa* and an *ekoddiṣṭa*, as there is for the evening.

[709028] And it should not be argued that because the first *muhūrta* is not prohibited again, the permission of its remaining part as secondary is understood.

[709180] Because by its capacity, it has been stated for the *vṛddhi śrāddha*.

[709251] The late morning, however, is not prohibited, nor is it praised.

[709317] Or, let it be that in an *ekoddiṣṭa*, *vṛddhi*, etc., the *śruti* on midday, morning, etc., is for the purpose of a rule, but the *śruti* on the afternoon in a *pārvaṇa* rite, by the previously cited verses, is only for the purpose of a great excellence.

[709573] And so, the *paryudāsa* of the night, etc., is proper.

[709629] And here, even though the time for a *pārvaṇa* is the afternoon, the commencement is in the *kutapa* itself.

[709739] > The *muhūrtas* of the day should be known as fifteen, always. / > Therein, the eighth *muhūrta*, that time is remembered as *kutapa*. // > Because at midday, the sun always becomes slow, / > therefore, the commencement there is distinguished as giving infinite fruit. //

[710013] {MV-S_236}

[710025] This is from the *Matsya Purāṇa*.

[710060] And this verse does not apply to an *ekoddiṣṭa*.

[710110] Because if it applied to that, the mention of 'commencement' would be meaningless.

[710194] And for this also, in the *pārvaṇa* rite, which is its modification, a regulation is remembered by Mārkaṇḍeya:

[710306] > In the forenoon of the bright fortnight, a wise man should perform the *śrāddha*. / > In the afternoon of the dark fortnight, however, he should not transgress the *rauhiṇa*. //

[710487] By Śūlapāṇi and others, however, this verse is written as being from the *Vāyu Purāṇa*.

[710576] This verse does not enjoin that for all rites related to the bright fortnight, such as the Yugādi, Manvādi, and annual *śrāddhas*, the time for the entire performance is the forenoon, and for those related to the dark fortnight, the afternoon.

[710821] Because in one case, it would lead to the undesirable consequence of the supersession of the afternoon, etc., and in the other, it is already obtained.

[710974] And it should not be argued that in the part on the dark fortnight, it is a restatement, and only in the part on the bright fortnight is there an injunction of the forenoon by the supersession of the afternoon obtained from the original model.

[711219] Because when it can be justified without supersession, that too is improper.

[711297] Therefore, it is established that by this verse, only the regulation of the time of commencement is made, by a difference of the fortnight, for the part before the *kutapa* which is included in the forenoon divided into two, and for the part after it which is included in such an afternoon.

[711589] By this very means, stating the meaning established by this verse in the Yugādis—

[711672] > Two in the bright, and two likewise in the dark, the poets know as Yugādis. / > The bright ones should be taken in the forenoon; and the dark ones, in the afternoon. //

[711844] —this verse of another *smṛti* is explained.

[711890] Others, however, explain these verses as they are heard.

[711948] Thus, this is the time for the *pārvaṇa* and *ekoddiṣṭa*.

[712007] In a *daivaśrāddha*, etc., however, from the verse, "A *daivika* *śrāddha* is in the forenoon," the forenoon should be taken.

[712134] And a *daivaśrāddha* is stated by Viśvāmitra, beginning with, "The *śrāddha* that is with the gods as the recipient, that is called *daivika*."

[712279] Here, by the word 'forenoon', the late morning is taken.

[712337] Because there is nothing to supersede the application of the prohibition of the morning time here as well.

[712445] Likewise, in "an *āmaśrāddha* is in the forenoon," here too, it is the same.

[712523] From the statement of Vyāghrapāda: "The *tithi* which is for three *muhūrtas* after the morning time, or a *tithi* of two *muhūrtas*, one should perform an *āmaśrāddha* there."

[712701] And this injunction of the forenoon in an *āmaśrāddha* applies only to an *āmaśrāddha* performed by a twice-born, not to an *āmaśrāddha* performed by a Śūdra.

[712861] Because in a performance during the day by a twice-born, since uncooked food is not fixed, and in a performance by a Śūdra, it is fixed, an injunction of the forenoon in both cases would lead to the undesirable consequence of a conflict between an obligatory and a non-obligatory connection.

[713154] And thus, the statement of Śātātapa, "The *kutapa* which is stated to be after midday, even there, what is given to the ancestors with uncooked food alone is a support," would also have a scope, says Hemādri.

[713364] That is not so.

[713381] {MV-S_237}

[713393] For the conflict of an obligatory connection would be either by the maxim of the *phalacamasa* (a. 3, pā. 5, adhi. 18), or by the maxim of the *saṃsthā* (a. 8, pā. 3, adhi. 4). Not the first, because the time of the forenoon, etc., does not have the quality of being a property of the substance, such as uncooked food, like the pressing of the Soma.

[713744] Not the second, because an *āmaśrāddha* is different from a *śrāddha* with cooked food.

[713833] The verse, however, if it has a source, then let it enjoin another time, as an option to the forenoon, in all *āmaśrāddhas*, but there is no special rule for a Śūdra's *āmaśrāddha*.

[714016] Therefore, an *āmaśrāddha* should be performed by a Śūdra also in the forenoon itself.

[714104] Likewise, from the verse, "The one occasioned by a *vṛddhi* is in the morning," the *nāndīśrāddha* other than the one for the birth of a son is to be performed in the morning.

[714281] In the birth of a son, however, due to the nature of the occasion, the time immediately after it is primary.

[714391] And here, the morning time is by the fivefold division, because in the other options, there is no division of the morning.

[714515] As for the use of the word 'morning' sometimes even for the first parts of a day divided into three or four, that is secondary, as was said in the determination of the festival day.

[714698] Thus ends the time for *śrāddha*.

[714733] ### [Prohibited Times for Offering Piṇḍas]

[714777] There, Pulastya says: > In the two solstices, a *śrāddha*, and in the two equinoxes, / > and on all the Yugādis, except for the offering of the *piṇḍa*. //

[714934] In the *Matsya Purāṇa*: > And on the *saṅkrāntis*, it should be performed, except for the offering of the *piṇḍa*.

[715050] Here, since the solstices, etc., are obtained by the mention of *saṅkrānti* alone, their mention again is for the sake of an excess of fault, says Hemādri.

[715207] Others, however, say it is a prohibition obtained by mistake.

[715270] In reality, however, the prohibition is justified by the difference of sages.

[715349] It is said by Vācaspati: "From the verse of the *Matsya Purāṇa*, 'From then on, on a *saṅkrānti* and on festival days such as an eclipse, one should perform a three-*piṇḍa* *śrāddha*; an *ekoddiṣṭa*, on the day of death,' a three-*piṇḍa* *śrāddha* should be performed even on a *saṅkrānti*, etc."

[715647] And it should not be argued that the prohibition in the said verse is inconsistent, because if it has a source, it can be justified by an option. That is not so.

[715810] Because the word 'three-*piṇḍa*' refers to a *pārvaṇa*.

[715867] Otherwise, if the number three were enjoined by restating the *piṇḍa*, it would lead to the undesirable consequence of a break in the context.

[716011] And it should not be argued that by the word 'three-*piṇḍa*', only the *piṇḍa* is enjoined, and the number three is a restatement by separating the parts.

[716167] Because in your view, since the prohibitive verse is without foundation, if the subordinate element in the form of the offering of the *piṇḍa*, which is obtained from the original model, is enjoined again, it would lead to the undesirable consequence of its being the first.

[716443] In the view that the offering of the *piṇḍa* is the principal rite, however, since that is established by the word *śrāddha* itself, if it is enjoined again, it would lead to the undesirable consequence of the exclusion of another principal rite in the form of the feeding of Brahmins.

[716730] And it should not be argued that the injunction of the offering of the *piṇḍa* is for the purpose of establishing its optional nature by accepting that the prohibition has a source.

[716913] {MV-S_238}

[716925] Because when the word 'three-*piṇḍa*' can be justified as being a subordinate element of the injunction of an *ekoddiṣṭa* by restating the *pārvaṇa* obtained from the original model, a split in the sentence and an option are improper.

[717161] In the *Brahma Purāṇa*, having read the verse of the *Matsya Purāṇa*, "And when a learned Brahmin, a knower of the Veda, who has established the sacred fires, comes to the house."

[717342] Having begun with the Trayodaśī of the dark fortnight of Bhādrapada conjoined with Maghā, the *Devī Purāṇa* says: > There too, a great worship should be performed for the deity of the ancestors. / > There, however, an eldest daughter should avoid the offering of the *piṇḍa*. //

[717622] Worship = in the form of a *śrāddha*. Deity of the ancestors = Maghā.

[717693] Bṛhatparāśara says: > And in Bharaṇī, one should not perform the offering of the *piṇḍa*.

[717784] And this is a prohibition of the offering of the *piṇḍa* in a *śrāddha* occasioned by Bharaṇī.

[717880] Because for it also, which is included in Mahālaya, its being a time for *śrāddha* is stated in the *Matsya Purāṇa*: > Bharaṇī in the fortnight of the ancestors is declared to be great. / > He by whom a *śrāddha* is performed on it becomes one who has performed the Gayā *śrāddha*. //

[718166] Likewise, in another *smṛti*: > And in a Gaṇḍa, Vaidhṛti, and Vyatīpāta, *piṇḍas* should be avoided by those who desire a son.

[718294] Here too, the prohibition is only in the *śrāddha* occasioned by each of those.

[718375] As for the verses: > On Sunday, on Tuesday, on Trayodaśī, on Nandā, Bhṛgu, and Maghā, / > one should not perform the offering of the *piṇḍa*, bathing with earth, or libation with sesame. //

[718566] Likewise, verses such as "On Nandā, Aśvinī, Kāma, Sunday, Tuesday, Bhṛgu, Agni, Pitṛ, and the *nakṣatra* of Kāla," cited in the *Prayogapārijāta*, apply to the option of performing it once, etc., enjoined for the dark fortnight of Mahālaya.

[718808] Some say that verses such as "On Nandā, on the day of Bhārgava, on Trayodaśī, in the three birth-stars, and on Revatī and Maghā, one should not perform the *āparapākṣika*," which prohibit the *śrāddha*, should be explained as prohibiting the *śrāddha* for the sake of syntactical unity.

[719096] In reality, however, since in the *Hemādri*, a *śrāddha* is enjoined on a *tithi* and a day of the week, the prohibition of the offering of the *piṇḍa* is only in the *śrāddha* occasioned by that.

[719294] For this very reason, they read: > Whatever fault is stated as occasioned by a *tithi* or a day of the week, / > that would be in a *śrāddha* occasioned by that, not in any other *śrāddha* ever. //

[719493] In the *Smṛtiratnāvalī*: > When a son is born, on a Vyatīpāta, and at an eclipse of the moon and sun, / > one should perform a *śrāddha* with effort, except for the offering of the *piṇḍa*. //

[719687] {MV-S_239}

[719699] And thus, the verse of the *Brahmavaivarta* cited by Hemādri, "When the sun is in Capricorn, at an eclipse of the moon and sun, the offering of the *piṇḍa* in the Gaṅgā is rare in the three worlds," applies to an eclipse in the Gaṅgā.

[719935] Kārṣṇājini says: > In a marriage, a vow, and a tonsure ceremony, for a year, half a year, and half of that, / > one should not perform the offering of the *piṇḍa*, bathing with earth, or libation with sesame. //

[720148] > In a *vṛddhi* rite alone, and elsewhere, the rejection of the offering of the *piṇḍa* / > has been made by Garga and other chief sages for one month for those who perform rites. //

[720332] And this applies to *sapiṇḍas* as well.

[720373] And so, Jyotiḥparāśara says: > When a marriage has been performed, the *sapiṇḍas* should avoid for twelve months the offering of the *piṇḍa*; in a sacred thread ceremony, for six months. //

[720564] An exception to this is in the same text: > In Mahālaya, in a Gayā *śrāddha*, on the death anniversary of one's mother and father, / > and likewise in the Sapiṇḍīkaraṇa of any mortal, // > even one who has performed a marriage should always perform the offering of the *piṇḍa*.

[720843] But surely, in the said verses, a prohibition of the offering of the *piṇḍa* is not possible.

[720938] Is this a simple prohibition (*pratiṣedha*) or a prohibitive exception (*paryudāsa*)?

[721025] Not the first. Because if the offering of the *piṇḍa*, which is obtained from scripture, is prohibited, it would lead to the undesirable consequence of an option (*vikalpa*), as in "he does not use those two for the animal."

[721251] Not the second. Because there is no syntactical unity with the injunction of each of those *śrāddhas*.

[721355] Here, the prohibition of the offering of the *piṇḍa*, which is heard after taking up many *śrāddhas* in a single verse, cannot be said to be read in the context of each of those *śrāddhas*.

[721546] And so, just as in "he does not use those two for the animal," since the injunction of the subordinate element is not in proximity, a *paryudāsa* is impossible, so too here, since the injunctions of each of those *śrāddhas*, which are enjoined by other *smṛtis*, are not in proximity, a *paryudāsa* is not possible.

[721863] Therefore, how can there be a cessation of the offering of the *piṇḍa* in the said *śrāddhas*? If this is so, no.

[721978] In verses such as "In the two solstices, a *śrāddha*, and in the two equinoxes," the offering of the *piṇḍa* is not excluded by restating the *śrāddha* enjoined by another *smṛti*, by which the said fault would have scope, but an origination of a new rite (*apūrvakarmotpattiḥ*) is made.

[722267] And because it is another *smṛti*, the injunction of what is already enjoined is not a fault.

[722362] {MV-S_240}

[722374] Therefore, since "except for the offering of the *piṇḍa*" can have syntactical unity with the injunction of each of those *śrāddhas* enjoined by its own verse, and its being a *paryudāsa* is justified, the cessation of the offering of the *piṇḍa* is indeed proper.

[722640] Thus ends the prohibited times for the offering of the *piṇḍa*.

[722705] > Thus ends the determination of the time for *śrāddha* in the *Samayaprakāśa* of the treatise named the glorious *Vīramitrodaya*, composed by the honorable Mitra Miśra—son of the honorable Paraśurāma Miśra, who was the son of the honorable Haṃsa Paṇḍita; who was prompted by the glorious King Vīrasiṃhadeva, who was the sun causing the lotus of the earth encircled by the four oceans to bloom, son of the glorious Mahārāja Madhukarasāha, who was the son of the glorious Mahārājādhirāja Pratāparudra; whose lotus feet are illuminated by the rays from the crest-jewels of the entire circle of vassal kings; who is a lion to the great elephant of the world's poverty, the very life of the learned, and proficient in crossing the ocean of all lores.

[755634] ### [Dharmas of the Yugas]

[755662] Now, the *dharmas* of the various *yugas*.

[755706] There, Pauṭhīnasi says:

[755731] > The *śruti*, and purity, and conduct differ in each age. / > Various *dharmas* arise for men in each *yuga*. //

[755846] The authority of *śruti*, etc., and the conduct of purity, etc., differ in each age, in each *yuga*.

[755948] By the first half of this verse, it is stated that in each *yuga*, the rites of the Agnihotra, etc., which are denoted by the word 'conduct', differ. And because of their difference, the difference of the authority of *śruti*, etc., which teaches them, is also stated. He states the reason for the difference of rites in the latter half: "Various *dharmas*," etc.

[756313] *Dharmas* are the absence of disease, etc. That is stated—

[756373] By Manu (a. 1, ślo. 83-84):

[756402] > Free from disease, with all their aims accomplished, and with a life of four hundred years, / > in Kṛta, Tretā, etc., however, their life diminishes by a quarter. //

[756571] > The life of mortals stated in the Veda, and the blessings of rites, / > bear fruit in each *yuga* in the world, and the powers of embodied beings. //

[756724] Thus it is said.

[756742] Likewise, in the Āraṇyaka Parvan, in the sight of Hanūmān, having cited the *dharmas* of Kṛta, etc., it is said:

[756856] > By one quarter, O son of Kuntī, *dharma* stands in the Kali Yuga. / > Having reached the tāmasic *yuga*, Keśava becomes dark. //

[756988] > The Vedic conduct ceases, and likewise the rites of *dharma* and sacrifice. /

[757069] {MV-S_256}

[757081] > Afflictions, diseases, lassitude, faults, anger, etc., / > and calamities arise, and anxieties, and hunger and fear. //

[757204] Thus it is said.

[757222] This is the meaning:

[757244] Due to the nature of the *yuga*, for the twice-born whose powers of knowledge, action, and the senses are diminishing day by day, because some subordinate elements of the rites of the Agnihotra, etc., are omitted due to carelessness, laziness, etc., and because they are not recognized as such, it is as if they are different, and it is as if the *śrutis*, etc., that teach them are also different. But there is no real difference in the *śrutis*, etc., or in the rites. This is the intention.

[757739] And in this same way, verses of this kind should be understood.

[757804] Manu also says (a. 1, ślo. 85-86):

[757840] > The *dharmas* are different in the Kṛta Yuga; in the Tretā, knowledge is said. / > They are different in the Kali Yuga for men, in accordance with the decline of the *yugas*. //

[758021] > Austerity (*tapas*) is supreme in the Kṛta Yuga; in the Tretā, knowledge is said. / > In the Dvāpara, they say only sacrifice; in the Kali Yuga, only charity. //

[758186] *Tapas* = the Kṛcchra, Cāndrāyaṇa, etc.

[758227] That is stated—

[758244] In the *Skanda Purāṇa*:

[758269] > The emaciation of the body by the Kṛcchra, Cāndrāyaṇa, etc., in the manner stated in the Veda, / > that is called *tapas* by the wise. //

[758410] Thus it is said.

[758428] 'Supreme' = primary.

[758450] In the *Mahābhārata*:

[758473] > Austerity is supreme in the Kṛta Yuga; in the Tretā, knowledge is the highest. / > In the Dvāpara, they say only sacrifice; in the Kali Yuga, only charity. //

[758635] Likewise:

[758646] > Austerity is supreme in the Kṛta Yuga; in the Tretā, knowledge is the highest. / > In the Dvāpara, they say only sacrifice; in Kali, charity, compassion, and self-control. //

[758824] Bṛhaspati says:

[758841] > Austerity is the *dharma* in the Kṛta Yuga; knowledge is established in the Tretā Yuga. / > In the Dvāpara, sacrifice is proclaimed; in Tiṣya, charity, compassion, and self-control. //

[759029] Tiṣya = Kali.

[759044] In the *Śivapurāṇa*:

[759066] > Meditation is supreme in the Kṛta Yuga; in the Tretā, the worship of the liṅga. / > In the Dvāpara, study; in Tiṣya, the praise of Mahādeva. //

[759213] Likewise:

[759224] > Meditation is supreme in the Kṛta Yuga; in the Tretā, sacrifice. / > In the Dvāpara, the worship of the liṅga; in Kali, the praise of Śaṅkara. //

[759373] In the *Viṣṇudharmottara*:

[759401] > Knowledge is supreme in the Kṛta Yuga; and in the Tretā, it is more so. /

[759478] {MV-S_257}

[759490] > And in the Dvāpara, likewise, sacrifice; consecration, however, is in the Kali Yuga. //

[759581] He states the authorities that are prevalent in the Kṛta, etc., *yugas*:

[759655] Parāśara says:

[759671] > In Kṛta, the *dharmas* are of Manu; in Tretā, those of Gautama are held. / > In Dvāpara, those of Śaṅkha and Likhita; in Kali, those of Parāśara are remembered. //

[759838] He states the reason for what was said, "Austerity is supreme in the Kṛta Yuga," etc.

[759925] The same author says:

[759948] > In Kṛta, the vital breaths are in the bones; in Tretā, they resort to the flesh. / > In Dvāpara, they are in the blood; in Kali, however, they are in food, etc. //

[760115] The vital breath is a specific wind with the five functions of breathing, etc. And that, bound by the thread of karma in bodies made of bone, flesh, etc., is situated in the bones, etc., by the power of the *yuga*.

[760331] He states that the verses that teach the nature of the *yuga*, etc., which are not useful for direct action and inaction, culminate in a specific prohibition:

[760491] The same author says:

[760514] > In each *yuga*, whatever *dharmas* there are, and whatever twice-born there are, / > their censure should not be made, for those twice-born are the very forms of the *yuga*. //

[760694] 'Of them' is for the sake of an example.

[760736] Having known all the faults, such as desire, anger, and greed, to be caused by time, one should not censure anyone. This is the meaning.

[760874] But surely, if this is so—

[760902] > *Dharma* is conquered by *adharma*, and truth by falsehood. / > And kings are conquered by spies, and men by women, in Kali. //

[761033] —if those who follow the *dharmas* of the *yuga*, which are taught by such verses, are not to be censured, then—

[761147] > Practice *dharma*, speak the truth.

[761186] > There is no *dharma* higher than truth, no sin greater than falsehood. / > Truth is indeed the foundation of *dharma*; therefore, one should not abandon the truth. //

[761356] —the scriptures of *śruti* and *smṛti*, which are the objects of injunctions and prohibitions, would be rendered without authority. If this is so, no.

[761508] Those who, in accordance with the *yuga*, having considered their own capacity, perform the obligatory and occasional rites by the primary or secondary rule, and avoid what is prohibited, and perform the penances for sins committed through carelessness, or those who are extremely unable, are not to be censured.

[761822] The others are to be censured. This is the true state of things.

[761888] Even so, one should not oneself reveal the faults of one who is in reality censurable. This is the intention of "their censure should not be made."

[762037] Bṛhaspati says:

[762054] > The *dharma* that would be in Kṛta from a year, that in Tretā is from three seasons. / > In Dvāpara, however, from three fortnights; in Kali, it would be in a day. //

[762224] {MV-S_258}

[762236] In the *Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa*:

[762264] > In Tretā, the *dharma* is of a year; in Dvāpara, it is remembered as of a month. / > A wise man, practicing according to his ability, obtains it in Kali in a day. //

[762433] In the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*:

[762457] > What is obtained in Kṛta in ten years, that in Tretā is in a year. / > In Dvāpara, that is in a month; in Kali, that is in a day and a night. //

[762605] In the *Skanda Purāṇa*:

[762630] > Brahmā is the deity in the Kṛta Yuga; in Tretā, the blessed Sun. / > In Dvāpara, the blessed Viṣṇu; in Kali, the god Maheśvara. //

[762764] > In Kṛta, Nārāyaṇa, subtle, of pure form, is worshipped. / > In Tretā, in the form of a sacrifice; in Dvāpara, by the Pāñcarātra. //

[762899] In the *Bhāgavata*:

[762920] > Kṛta, Tretā, and Dvāpara, and Kali—in these, Keśava / > is worshipped by various methods, with various colors, names, and forms. //

[763055] > In Kṛta, he is white, with four arms, with matted hair, wearing bark garments, / > with a black antelope skin and a sacred thread, and a bright staff and water-pot. //

[763226] > Men then are tranquil, without enmity, friendly, and equal. / > They worship the god with austerity, and with tranquility and self-control. //

[763372] > He is called Haṃsa, Anuvarṇa, Vaikuṇṭha, Varya, Yogeśvara, Amala, / > Īśvara, Puruṣa, Asakta, and Paramātman. //

[763488] > In Tretā, he is of red color, with four arms, with three girdles, / > with golden hair, the soul of the three Vedas, and marked by the sacrificial ladle and spoon, etc. //

[763663] > Then men, devoted to *dharma* and speakers of Brahman, worship that god, / > Hari, who is the form of all the gods, with the knowledge of the three Vedas. //

[763824] > He is called Viṣṇu, Yajña, Pṛśnigarbha, Sarvadeva, Urukrama, / > Vṛṣākapi, Jayanta, and Urugāya. //

[763927] > In Dvāpara, the blessed one is dark, with yellow garments, with his own weapons, / > and marked with the Śrīvatsa, etc., and with other signs. //

[764076] > Then mortals, O king, who desire to know the supreme, worship that Puruṣa, / > who is marked as a great king, with the Veda and the Tantra. //

[764222] > Salutation to you, Vāsudeva; salutation to Saṅkarṣaṇa. / > To Pradyumna and Aniruddha, salutation to you, O blessed one! //

[764349] > To Nārāyaṇa, the ṛṣi, the Puruṣa, the great soul, / > to the lord of the universe, to the universe, to the soul of all beings, salutation! //

[764494] {MV-S_259}

[764506] > Thus in Dvāpara, they praise the lord of the earth, the lord of the world. //

[764587] > And in Kali also, by the method of various Tantras, as you should hear. / > Kṛṣṇa, of dark color, with his limbs, subordinate limbs, weapons, and companions, // > the wise worship with sacrifices that are mostly in the form of chanting. / > Thus, in accordance with the *yuga*, the blessed one, the lord of blessings, Hari, / > is worshipped by men who follow the *yuga*, O king!

[764970] In the *Viṣṇudharmottara*:

[764998] > Puṣkara should be served in Kṛta; in Tretā, Naimiṣa. / > In Dvāpara, however, Kurukṣetra; in Kali, one should resort to the Gaṅgā. //

[765135] Vāmadeva says:

[765151] > In Kṛta, all sacred fords; in Tretā, Puṣkara is supreme. / > In Dvāpara, however, Kurukṣetra; in Kali, the Gaṅgā is distinguished. //

[765288] Thus end the *dharmas* of the various *yugas*.

[765336] ### [Things to be Avoided in the Various Yugas]

[765385] Now, the things to be avoided in the various *yugas*.

[765440] There, Śātātapa says:

[765463] > In Kṛta, one should abandon the country; in Tretā, one should abandon the village. / > In Dvāpara, one family; in Kali, however, the agent. //

[765609] > In Kṛta, by mere conversation; in Tretā, by touching. / > In Dvāpara, by taking food; in Kali, one falls by the act. //

[765732] Thus end the things to be avoided in the various *yugas*.

[765791] ### [Dharmas of the Kali Yuga]

[765823] Now, the *dharmas* of the Kali Yuga.

[765861] In the *Mahābhārata*:

[765884] > He who, with this mantra, "Oṃ namaḥ Śivāya," / > worships Śaṅkara once, is freed from all sins. //

[765986] > In whatever state he may be, or joined with all sins, / > he who says "Oṃ namaḥ Śivāya" is freed in Kali. //

[766098] > Even a salutation offered with deceit to the wielder of the trident / > is the destroyer of the multitude of faults of worldly existence in Kali. //

[766250] Likewise:

[766261] > He who always worships him with faith, O best of sages, / > in a liṅga or on a prepared ground, or in a man-made one, according to the rules, // > having conquered the fault of the *yuga*, rejoices in the world of Rudra.

[766485] In the *Liṅgapurāṇa*:

[766508] > In Kali, Rudra, Mahādeva, Śaṅkara, Nīlalohita, /

[766560] {MV-S_260}

[766572] > manifests himself in a disfigured form for the sake of the establishment of *dharma*. // > Those Brahmins who serve that Śaṅkara in any way, / > having conquered the fault of Kali, go to the supreme state. //

[766784] Vyāsa says:

[766797] > What one obtains in Kṛta by meditating, in Tretā by sacrificing with sacrifices, and in Dvāpara by worshipping, / > that one obtains in Kali by chanting the name of Keśava. //

[766976] In the *Bhāgavata*:

[766997] > The noble, who know the qualities and partake of the essence, praise Kali, / > where by chanting alone, every desired object is obtained. //

[767141] 'By chanting' means by the chanting of Hari.

[767187] > There is no higher gain than this for embodied beings who are wandering here, / > from which one would find the supreme peace, and worldly existence is destroyed. //

[767356] In the *Agnipurāṇa*:

[767378] > There is nothing more conducive to the welfare of men than the worship of Viṣṇu, O sage, / > in this tāmasic, terrible *yuga*, which is devoid of sacrifices and gods. //

[767551] > One should perform the worship of Vāsudeva in the Kali Yuga, O king. / > The fruit that a man obtained in Kṛta by worshipping Hari with devotion for a hundred years, according to the rules, / > that he obtains in Kali in a day and a night. //

[767797] Likewise:

[767808] > Those men who, in Kali, which destroys the impurity of Kali, worship Hari, who removes all sins, / > they too are to be venerated, like Hari. //

[767956] > A man obtains a great excellence of *dharma* in Kali. / > With little effort, O knower of *dharma*, I am pleased with Kali for that reason. //

[768102] > Blessed are the Brahmins in Kali; with little effort, there is a great fruit. //

[768186] In the *Viṣṇupurāṇa*:

[768209] > Cities full of temples of the gods, in the Kali Yuga, / > should be made by kings who desire the world of heaven. //

[768329] In the *Kūrmapurāṇa*:

[768352] > One should serve the Gaṅgā alone, and especially at Prayāga. / > There is nothing else to destroy the very difficult impurity born of the Kali Yuga. //

[768507] In the *Skanda* and *Bhaviṣya Purāṇas*:

[768548] > If a man desires the fruit of worldly enjoyment and liberation with little effort, / > a wise man should resort to sacred fords, and in Kali, especially the Gaṅgā. //

[768718] > The Gaṅgā flowing north in Kāśī, and my liṅga of Viśveśvara, / > both are givers of liberation to men, when the forest fire of Kali has arrived. //

[768869] {MV-S_261}

[768881] In the *Nāradīya*:

[768901] > In Kali, for the swift attainment of that supreme Brahman by men, / > the great sages say that the service of the Gaṅgā is the great means. //

[769047] In the *Kāmikasaṃhitā*:

[769072] > There would be no success of the Vedic mantras in one who is devoid of purity. / > Without mantras, the wise do not succeed; purity is difficult to obtain. //

[769234] > In the age of Kali, especially, a pure day is not seen. / > In the Kali Yuga, when *dharma* is destroyed by darkness, and which is terrible, // > the thread of *dharma* with an unbroken continuity is indeed the Jāhnavī. / > Without the Gaṅgā, which is the form of *dharma*, how would there be a path in Kali? /

[769548] > The cutting off of his head, or even the giving up of his life, is better / > for him who, being able, does not go to the Gaṅgā in the age of Kali. //

[769702] In the *Bhaviṣyapurāṇa*:

[769728] > For those of impure mind in Kali, whose souls are attached to sinful substances, / > and whose rites are without rules, there is no path without the Gaṅgā. //

[769890] > He who, without resorting to the Gaṅgā, desires liberation in Kali, / > is like one born blind who endeavors to see the sun here. //

[770026] > Vain is his family, vain his knowledge, vain his sacrifices, vain his austerities, / > vain are his gifts here, who does not go to the Gaṅgā in Kali. //

[770182] Thus end the *dharmas* of the Kali Yuga.

[770224] ### [Things to be Avoided in the Kali Yuga]

[770269] Now, the things to be avoided in the Kali Yuga (*kaliyugavarjyāni*).

[770339] In the *Brahmapurāṇa*:

[770363] > Long-term celibacy, and the carrying of a water-pot, / > marriage to one of the same *gotra* or a *sapiṇḍa* of the mother, and cow-slaughter, // > the human and horse sacrifices, alcohol, are to be avoided in Kali by the twice-born. //

[770602] Likewise:

[770613] > The re-marriage of a married woman, the eldest's share, and cow-slaughter, / > these five one should not do in Kali: a brother's wife, and a water-pot. //

[770771] Kratu says:

[770784] > The procreation of a son from a brother-in-law (*devara*), a girl once given is not given again, / > cow-slaughter should not be done in a sacrifice, and in Kali, no water-pot. //

[770967] In the *Ādityapurāṇa*:

[770991] > Oaths, omens, dreams, palmistry, and what is overheard, / > what is obtained by request, and commands, sometimes occur in Kali. //

[771125] {MV-S_262}

[771137] > Therefore, by the mere gain of that, one should not perform the rite that is to be done.

[771229] Likewise, by the force of the convention of the knowers of *dharma*, other things also are to be avoided in Kali.

[771344] * The appointment of a brother-in-law for procreation with a widow. * The union of a girl whose virginity is not broken with another suitor. * The marriage of girls of unequal caste by the twice-born. * The killing of an attacking Brahmin aggressor in a righteous battle. * The acceptance of a twice-born who has traveled by sea in a ship, even if he has been purified. * The consecration for a *satra* sacrifice for all. * The carrying of a water-pot. * The great journey to death (*mahāprasthānagamana*). * The slaughter of a cow in a *gosava* sacrifice. * The acceptance of a cup of *surā* even in a Sautrāmaṇī sacrifice. * The licking of the sacrificial ladle (*havanī*) in the Agnihotra, and the acceptance of what has been licked. * The entry into the forest-dweller (*vānaprastha*) āśrama according to the rules. * The reduction of sin in proportion to one's conduct and study. * The prescription of a penance ending in death for Brahmins. * The fault of contact, and the penance for great sins other than theft. * The act of offering an animal for a distinguished guest, an honored guest, and the ancestors. * The acceptance as a son of those other than a given (*datta*) and a natural (*aurasa*) son. * Contact with those of the same caste who are likewise uncorrupted, even if they have been purified.[^1] * The abandonment of a guru's wife in case of sexual union in an improper place. * The abandonment of one's life for the sake of another. * The avoidance of what has been left over. * The resolve for the sake of the worship of an image, which is with its *dharmas*. * The touching of the limbs after the collection of the bones. * The act of the *śāmitra* priest for Brahmins, and the selling of Soma. * The taking of food by fasting for six meals from one of low karma. * The edibility of the food of a householder for Śūdras who are servants, cowherds, of the same family, friends, and share-croppers. * The service of sacred fords from a great distance. * The practice of a disciple behaving towards the guru's wives as towards the guru. * The livelihood in times of distress for the best of the twice-born, and the non-stealing of horses. * The taking of the fire-sticks for the sake of offspring by the best of the twice-born. * The traveling of Brahmins, and the act of blowing on the mouth-fire. * The acceptance of a woman defiled by force, etc., as enjoined by the rules.

[773854] {MV-S_263}

[773866] * The practice of begging for alms from all castes by an ascetic, according to the rules. * The ten days in new water, and the gift (*dakṣiṇā*) prompted by the guru. * The act of cooking, etc., by a Śūdra for Brahmins, etc. * And the death of the elderly, etc., by falling from a cliff or into a fire. * The act of sipping water by the remaining ones from the milk left over after the satisfaction of the *gotra*. * The prescription of punishment for witnesses in a dispute between a father and a son. * The evening-dwelling of an ascetic, by sages who are intent on the truth. * These rites were made to cease by the great-souled ones at the beginning of Kali for the sake of the protection of the world, / > with a systematic arrangement, by the wise. / > And the convention of the good should be an authority like the Veda. //

[774719] > The re-marriage of a married woman, the eldest's share, and cow-slaughter, / > these five one should not do in Kali: a brother's wife, and a water-pot. //

[774877] In the *Nigama* also:

[774900] > An uninjured cow, and meat in a *śrāddha*, and honey, / > the procreation of a son from a brother-in-law—these five one should avoid in Kali. //

[775048] Vyāsa says:

[775061] > When four thousand and four hundred years of Kali have passed, / > then there will be the acceptance of Tretā. //

[775178] > Renunciation should not be performed by a knowing Brahmin.

[775240] Devala says:

[775254] > As long as the division of the castes exists, as long as the Veda is current, / > the Agnihotra and renunciation, one should perform them in the Kali Yuga. //

[775416] Laugākṣi says:

[775432] > The separate establishment of the *śrauta* and *smārta* fires is remembered as *ardhādhāna*. / > The establishment of them all, the act of uniting them, is based on the preceding *yuga*. //

[775625] Thus end the things to be avoided in Kali.

[775669] ### [The Time for Initiation (Dīkṣā)]

[775708] Now, the time for initiation (*dīkṣā*).

[775749] In the *Kālottara*:

[775770] > The conjunction of autumn and spring is remembered in the rite of the *dīkṣā* ceremony. / > In their absence, the rainy season is praised, but not at other times. //

[775939] 'The conjunction of autumn and spring' means in autumn and in spring.

[776010] There, the fruits of the months are in the *Saṃhitā*:

[776065] > Non-success, attainment of land, death, destruction of relatives, /

[776136] {MV-S_264}

[776148] > increase of life, destruction of offspring, attainment of wealth, and accumulation of gems, // > attainment of the auspicious, destruction of one's place, and intelligence, wealth, and subjugation. / > This should be known as the fruit of Caitra, etc.; one should avoid the intercalary month. //

[776447] In the *Kāraṇa*, however, the fruits of the months are stated differently:

[776523] > In the month before Vaiśākha, the commencement of a mantra is very difficult. / > In Vaiśākha, it is a giver of wealth; in Jyeṣṭha, it becomes a bestower of death. //

[776693] > In Āṣāḍha, it is for the gain of a son; in Śrāvaṇa, it is considered auspicious. / > And in Bhādra, there is a loss of knowledge, and likewise, non-success is proclaimed. //

[776870] > In Āśvina, all success; Kārttika is a giver of the success of knowledge. / > Mārgaśīrṣa is a doer of good; Pauṣa is a producer of sorrow. //

[777014] > In Māgha, an increase of intelligence; in Phālguna, all subjugation. //

[777089] Thus it is said.

[777107] Here, since the good fruit of Āṣāḍha is stated, and in the preceding verse, an undesirable fruit is stated, there is an option.

[777236] In the *Siddhāntaśekhara*, however, there is a special rule:

[777298] > In the autumn season and in Vaiśākha, *dīkṣā* gives the best fruit. / > In Phālguna, Mārgaśīrṣa, and Jyeṣṭha, the *dīkṣā* is middling. //

[777439] > Āṣāḍha, Śrāvaṇa, and Māgha are inferior, but are resorted to by the good. / > The month of Caitra is condemned, and likewise Pauṣa and Bhādrapada. //

[777592] > Even in the condemned months, *dīkṣā* is said to be auspicious during an eclipse.

[777677] In the *Kāraṇa*:

[777695] > In the month before Āṣāḍha, and in Āṣāḍha and Mārgaśīrṣa, / > a wise man should not perform *dīkṣā*; in other months, he may. //

[777827] > In the bright fortnight, in an auspicious lunar mansion, and on an auspicious day of the week and *tithi*. //

[777940] Thus it is said.

[777958] In the *Kriyākāṇḍaśekhara*, however:

[777996] > There should be no *dīkṣā* without a festival day, in the rainy season, and in Madhu and Pauṣa. / > At other times, it should always be performed, when the guru and the disciple are pure. //

[778190] > At the beginning of the rainy season, having observed the auspicious signs, /

[778271] Thus, even at times prohibited at the beginning of the rainy season, if auspicious signs, etc., are observed, *dīkṣā* should be performed, says Hemādri.

[778425] In the *Agastisaṃhitā*:

[778450] > In the bright fortnight or in the dark, *dīkṣā* brings all happiness. //

[778526] Thus it is said.

[778544] In the *Kālottara*, there is a special rule:

[778590] > The commencement of *dīkṣā* should be performed by those who desire worldly prosperity in the bright fortnight. / > By those who desire liberation, in the dark fortnight. //

[778767] Thus it is said.

[778785] {MV-S_265}

[778797] In the *Saṃhitā*, however:
