WORK: Carmen Saeculare  (slug: horace-carmen-saeculare-525e9c)

CHAPTER: 01-carmen-saeculare



[0] 1. Phoebus, and Diana, mistress of the woodlands, bright glory of the sky, O ever to be worshipped and ever worshipped, grant what we pray for at this sacred season,

[167] at which the Sibylline verses have admonished chosen maidens and unblemished boys to sing a hymn to the gods to whom the seven hills are dear.

[311] Kindly Sun, who in your shining chariot bring forth the day and hide it, and rise again another and yet the same — may you never look upon anything greater than the city of Rome!

[491] Gentle to bring to term the births now ripe, Ilithyia, watch over our mothers, whether you choose to be called Lucina or Genitalis:

[624] Goddess, raise up our offspring, and prosper the decrees of the senators upon the wedding of women and the marriage law, fruitful of new children,

[772] so that the fixed cycle of ten times eleven years may bring back the songs and the games, thronged three times by clear day and as often by welcome night.

[928] And you, Fates, truthful in your singing of what has once been spoken — and let the steadfast boundary of things preserve it — to fortunes now accomplished join good fortunes still to come.

[1119] May the earth, rich in crops and cattle, present Ceres with a crown of grain, and may healthful waters and the breezes of Jove nourish the new growth.

[1271] Mild and gentle, your weapon laid aside, hear the suppliant boys, Apollo; queen of the stars, two-horned, hear, O Moon, the girls:

[1403] if Rome is your work, and if Trojan companies held the Etruscan shore, that band bidden to change their homes and city in a saving voyage,

[1543] for whom, through burning Troy, with no treachery, chaste Aeneas, surviving his fatherland, made ready a free road, destined to give more than was left behind:

[1704] Gods, grant to the teachable young an upright character, gods, grant to peaceful old age its rest, and to the race of Romulus give wealth and offspring and every glory.

[1874] And whatever the renowned blood of Anchises and of Venus entreats of you with white oxen, let him obtain it — mightier than the warring foe, gentle to the one laid low.

[2044] Now the Mede fears our hands, powerful by sea and land, and the Alban axes; now the Scythians, lately so proud, and the Indians seek our answers.

[2191] Now Faith and Peace and Honour and old-fashioned Modesty, and Virtue too long neglected, dare to return, and blessed Plenty appears with her full horn.

[2344] May Phoebus, the augur, splendid with his gleaming bow and dear to the nine Muses, who by his healing art relieves the weary limbs of the body,

[2489] if he looks with favour upon the Palatine heights, prolong the Roman state and prosperous Latium into another cycle and ever to a better age.

[2632] And may Diana, who holds the Aventine and Algidus, heed the prayers of the fifteen men, and lend friendly ears to the vows of the boys.

[2769] That Jove and all the gods feel thus, I carry home a good and certain hope, I, a chorus trained to sing the praises of Phoebus and Diana.
