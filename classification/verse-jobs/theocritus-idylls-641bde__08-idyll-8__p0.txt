CHAPTER: 08-idyll-8



[0] 8. The Singing-Match: Daphnis and Menalcas With the graceful Daphnis, as he herded his cattle,

[96] Menalcas met, pasturing his sheep, they say, on the long hills.

[161] Both of them were red-haired, both still beardless,

[214] both skilled in piping, both in singing.

[256] And Menalcas, looking at Daphnis, was the first to speak:

[315] Daphnis, guardian of the lowing cattle, will you sing against me?

[382] I say I will beat you, singing as long as I choose.

[435] And Daphnis answered him with these words:

[479] Menalcas, shepherd of the woolly sheep, you piper,

[531] you will never beat me, however you may strain at singing.

[591] Menalcas

[601] Then do you care to put it to the test? Do you care to set up a prize?

[673] Daphnis

[682] I care to put it to the test; I care to set up a prize.

[739] Menalcas

[749] And what shall we stake that would be worthy of us?

[802] Daphnis

[811] I will stake a calf; do you stake a lamb as big as its mother.

[875] Menalcas

[885] I will never stake a lamb, for my father is harsh,

[937] and my mother too, and they count all the sheep at evening.

[998] Daphnis

[1007] But what then will you stake? What more shall the winner have?

[1071] Menalcas

[1081] A pipe that I made, a fair one of nine reeds,

[1128] set with white wax, even below and even above—

[1176] this I will stake; but I will not stake my father's goods.

[1236] Daphnis

[1245] Why, I too have a pipe of nine reeds,

[1284] set with white wax, even below and even above.

[1332] I fitted it together the other day; and I still feel a pain

[1393] in this finger, where a reed split and cut me.

[1441] Menalcas

[1451] But who shall judge between us? Who shall hear us?

[1503] Daphnis

[1512] What if we call that goatherd over here,

[1554] the one at whose kids the white-spotted dog is barking?

[1611] So the boys shouted, and the goatherd came to listen.

[1666] The boys sang, and the goatherd was willing to judge.

[1721] And first, having drawn the lot, the loud-voiced Menalcas sang,

[1786] then Daphnis took up the answering song.

[1828] Menalcas

[1838] You glens and rivers, race divine, if ever Menalcas

[1891] the piper sang you a song you loved,

[1929] graze his ewe-lambs with a good will; and if ever Daphnis

[1988] come with his heifers, may he have no less.

[2033] Daphnis

[2042] You springs and pastures, sweet growth, if Daphnis

[2094] makes music like the nightingales,

[2130] fatten this herd of mine; and if Menalcas

[2173] brings any here, may he too feed all in plenty, and rejoice.

[2235] Menalcas

[2245] Here are the ewes, here the goats that bear twins, here the bees

[2311] fill their hives, and here the oaks grow taller,

[2361] here the fair Milon sets his feet; but if he goes away,

[2418] then dry are both the shepherd and the pastures there.

[2474] Daphnis

[2483] Spring everywhere, everywhere the meadows, everywhere with milk

[2548] the udders swell, and the young are reared,

[2593] wherever the fair girl walks; but if she goes away,

[2646] then withered are both the cowherd and his cattle.

[2698] Menalcas

[2708] O he-goat, husband of the white she-goats, O depth of the woodland

[2776] without end—(O snub-nosed kids, come here to the water)—

[2834] for there he is; go, you stub-horned one, and say: Milon,

[2893] even Proteus, though a god, pastured his seals.

[2942] Daphnis

[2951] May I not have the land of Pelops, nor talents of gold

[3007] to possess, nor to outrun the winds;

[3045] but under this rock will I sing, holding you in my arms,

[3103] watching the flocks graze together, toward the Sicilian sea.

[3165] Daphnis

[3174] For the trees winter is a dread evil, for the waters drought,

[3237] for the birds the snare, for the wild beasts the net,

[3292] for a man the longing for a tender maiden. O father, O Zeus,

[3354] I am not the only one to have loved; you too love women.

[3412] So the boys sang in answering strains;

[3452] and Menalcas led off the last song in this way.

[3501] Spare my kids, wolf, spare my mothers,

[3541] and do me no wrong, though I am small and tend so many.

[3598] O Lampurus, my dog, are you held by so deep a sleep?

[3652] You should not sleep deeply when you herd with a boy.

[3707] And you, my ewes, do not be slow to feed your fill on the soft

[3771] grass; you will not tire, for it will grow again.

[3822] Hsst, graze, graze, and fill your udders, all of you,

[3877] that the lambs may have part, and part I may lay by in the baskets.

[3946] Then Daphnis in his turn struck up clear-voiced to sing:

[4004] And me too, the other day, a girl with meeting brows, looking from the cave

[4081] as I drove my heifers past, called fair, fair;

[4129] but I gave her no answer back, not even a bitter one,

[4184] but looking down I went on my own way.

[4224] Sweet is the heifer's voice, sweet her breath;

[4272] and sweet in summer to lie in the open air by running water.

[4334] For the oak the acorns are an ornament, for the apple-tree the apples,

[4406] for the cow the calf, for the cowherd the cattle themselves.

[4468] So the boys sang, and the goatherd spoke thus:

[4516] Sweet is your mouth, Daphnis, and lovely your voice.

[4570] Better to hear you singing than to lick honey.

[4618] Take the pipes; for you have won in the singing.

[4668] And if you are willing to teach me too, who herd goats beside you,

[4736] I will give you that hornless goat as your teaching-fee,

[4794] who always fills the pail above the brim.

[4837] As the boy rejoiced and leaped and clapped his hands,

[4892] having won, so might a fawn leap to its mother;

[4941] and as the other smoldered and was overturned in his heart with grief,

[5013] so might a bride be grieved when she is wed against her will.

[5076] And from that time Daphnis was first among the shepherds,

[5135] and, while still in his early youth, he wed the Naiad nymph.
