[105927] When he had ceased from the race-courses upon a glorious victory, the emperor again set Uranius upon the prize-winning chariots, bestowing on the people a favour; for the city does not long for Uranius apart from contests. Therefore, driving a second time, both of his last victory and of his former, it set him up in admiration.

[106258] 378. Anonymous Epigrammatists

[106289] Uranius has Nicaea near, and younger Rome, of the one being a son, in the other winning glory. And he conquers on both sides, since he was ambidextrous both this way and that, to run ahead or to drive past. Therefore in golden metal too they inscribed him, in the most glorious of possessions a glorious charioteer.

[106606] 379. Thomas

[106619] On Anastasius The bold charioteer, forgetful of the chariot's contests, Anastasius, the ground here holds him lying, who bound on so many crowns before, as others of the charioteers have seen days of racing.

[106828] 380. Anonymous Epigrammatists

[106859] On Porphyrius, of the Blue faction Having mastered every charioteer on earth, he was fitly raised to run toward the upper air, Porphyrius, the wonder of the people of the Blues. For this man, conquering every charioteer on earth, rises up, that he may run even with the sun.

[107135] 381. Anonymous Epigrammatists

[107166] The first down blooming on him, this Porphyrius, son of Calchas, drew the Blue reins. And I am amazed how some hand paints living horses of his. For if it strike again, I think, it will run to find victory once more.

[107384] 382. Anonymous Epigrammatists

[107415] On Faustinus, of the Green faction Behold the play of the architect of the building; for if it had not been roofed with a strong cover, toward the heavens Faustinus would have risen, running as though alive with his horses, once the glory of the Greens. Lift off the roof, and he reaches the upper air.

[107719] 383. Anonymous Epigrammatists

[107750] This is Faustinus, the former charioteer, whom the people of the Green faction, finding him, knew nothing at all of defeat in the race. For he was an old man, as you see; but his strength was that of some youth, in no way at all defeated.

[107990] 384. Anonymous Epigrammatists

[108021] On Constantine, charioteer of the Whites Constantine, drawing the reins of the White, if he had not been pent in by the strength of the building, would have conquered the three, first reaching the upper air. Without breath you have seen one who runs the air; art persuades me to behold this man breathing.

[108328] 385. Anonymous Epigrammatists

[108359] Constantine he was; but in the days of old he drew nimbly the four-horse team of white colour. But since Charon snatched this man away, the light of the contest of horse-races set, and all the delight of the theatre, and the art.

[108590] 386. Anonymous Epigrammatists

[108621] On Julian, charioteer of the Reds The hand knows how to beget those long since dead. For Julian too is as strong as of old, drawing and back-drawing the reins of the Red; and now, painted, he stands on high with his chariot; the gesture and the hand remain; give him the turning-post.

[108907] 387. Anonymous Epigrammatists

[108938] This Julian, holding the chariot of the Red, conquered his opponents in the race. But if the painter had granted him also the grace of breath, he is ready, a charioteer once more, both to come first, and to take even the crown.
