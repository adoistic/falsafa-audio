[0] 6. The Singing-Match: Damoetas and Daphnis Damoetas and Daphnis the cowherd once drove their herd

[99] together into one place, Aratus; and one of them

[149] was red-haired, the other half-bearded; and sitting both by a spring

[219] at midday in the summer, they sang in this way.

[268] Daphnis began first, for it was he who first challenged.

[326] Galatea pelts your flock, Polyphemus,

[365] with apples, calling you a laggard in love, a goatherd;

[422] and you do not even look at her, poor wretch, poor wretch, but sit

[490] piping sweetly. Look, again she pelts the dog,

[538] which follows as guardian of your sheep; and it barks,

[594] gazing toward the sea, and the fair waves show it

[645] softly plashing as it runs along the shore.

[690] Take care, lest she leap upon the girl's legs

[737] as she comes from the sea, and tear her lovely skin.

[791] But she even from there coquets with you, light

[840] as the dry thistledown when the fair summer parches it;

[897] and she flees the one who loves her, and pursues the one who does not,

[969] and moves her last counter on the line; for to a lover,

[1026] Polyphemus, things not fair often appear fair.

[1074] After him Damoetas struck up and sang as follows.

[1125] I saw her, by Pan, when she was pelting the flock,

[1177] and she did not escape my notice, no, by this one sweet eye of mine, with which I shall see

[1270] to the end; but as for the seer Telemus, who speaks ill words,

[1334] let him bear his ill words home, to keep them for his children.

[1399] Yet I myself, to tease her in turn, do not look at her,

[1456] but say I have some other woman; and she, hearing this,

[1513] grows jealous of me, by the Healer, and pines away, and from the sea

[1583] she peers out in a frenzy at the caves and at the flocks.

[1642] And I hissed the dog to bark at her; for when I loved her,

[1702] it would whimper, laying its muzzle against her flank.

[1758] Seeing me do this often, perhaps she will send

[1806] a messenger. But I will bar my doors, until she swears

[1862] to spread a fair bed for me herself upon this island.

[1917] For indeed I am not so ill to look at as they say.

[1969] The other day I gazed into the sea, when it was calm,

[2024] and fair my beard, and fair my one eye seemed,

[2072] so far as I could judge, and the gleam of my teeth

[2124] showed whiter than the marble of Paros.

[2165] And, lest I be bewitched, I spat three times into my bosom;

[2226] for so the old woman Cotyttaris taught me.

[2270] Having said so much, Damoetas kissed Daphnis,

[2317] and the one gave him a pipe, the other a fine flute.

[2371] Damoetas played the flute, and Daphnis the cowherd piped,

[2430] and at once the heifers danced upon the soft grass.

[2483] But neither was the victor, for they came out unbeaten.
