WORK: On Style  (slug: demetrius-of-phaleron-on-style-4c75b6)

CHAPTER: 01-on-style



[110541] 302. Beside the forceful style too there lies, as is natural, a faulty one, called the graceless. It arises in the things, when one says shameful and unspeakable things openly, as the man accusing Timandra of having played the harlot heaped upon the court the basin and the obols and the rush mat and much such foul language.

[110868] 303. The composition appears graceless if it resembles something torn apart, as the man who said, "Things being thus, this and that — to kill." And when the clauses have no connection with one another, but resemble things broken off. And periods that are continuous and long and stifle the speakers are not only cloying but also unpleasant.

[111210] 304. By the naming, too, things that are graceful often appear more unpleasant, as Cleitarchus, speaking of the wasp, an animal resembling a bee: "It grazes, he says, the mountain country, and flies into the hollow oaks" — as though speaking of a wild ox or the Erymanthian boar, and not of some bee, so that the discourse becomes at once graceless and frigid. And both of these somehow lie close to one another.
