WORK: Liber Peristephanon  (slug: aurelius-prudentius-clem-liber-peristephanon-42bcd7)

CHAPTER: 01-hymn-1



[0] 1. Hymn in Honour of the Holy Martyrs Emeterius and Chelidonius of Calahorra.

[79] The names of two martyrs are written in heaven,

[128] which Christ has inscribed there in letters of gold,

[182] and the same he has given to earth, written in marks of blood.

[246] By this lineage the blessed Spanish land flourishes the world over,

[315] this place God saw as worthy to hold their bones,

[366] a chaste host fit to keep their blessed bodies.

[415] Here, stained by a twofold slaughter, the ground drank the warm streams,

[489] and now the dwellers throng the sands steeped in holy gore,

[550] imploring with voice, with vows, with offerings.

[600] From abroad too the settler of the world comes hither,

[656] for rumour, the betrayer, has run through every land

[710] that here are patrons of mankind whom suppliants may surround.

[774] No one here, in his asking, has ever heaped up pure prayers in vain;

[844] glad the suppliant returns from here, his weeping wiped away,

[907] feeling that all he justly asked for has been granted.

[963] So great is the care of these advocates for our perils,

[1020] they let no one pour out a single murmur of the voice in vain,

[1084] but hear it, and at once bear it to the ear of the eternal King.

[1150] Thence from the very fountainhead gifts flow in abundance to the earth,

[1223] which water the suppliants’ causes with the remedies they sought.

[1290] Never has good Christ denied anything to his own witnesses,

[1351] witnesses whom neither chains nor cruel death could frighten

[1413] from confessing the one God at the cost of their blood—

[1470] blood, yet a longer light repays so great a loss.

[1521] This kind of death is glorious, this worthy of upright men:

[1582] to give to the enemy’s sword the limbs that disease would have devoured,

[1656] woven of failing veins, and by death to conquer the foe.

[1714] A beautiful thing it is to suffer the stroke beneath the persecutor’s blade.

[1792] Through the wide wound a noble gate is opened to the just:

[1852] the soul, washed in the red fount, leaps out from its seat in the heart.

[1926] Nor had they led, before, a life untried by hard toil,

[1982] these soldiers whom Christ calls to the everlasting belt of service;

[2052] their valour, schooled in war and arms, now fights in sacred shrines.

[2123] They leave the standards of Caesar, they choose the sign of the cross,

[2195] and in place of the windblown banners of the dragons they once bore

[2264] they carry the glorious wood before them, the wood that crushed the dragon.

[2341] They count it base to bear javelins in their ready right hands,

[2406] to batter a wall with engines, to ring a camp with trenches,

[2468] to stain impious hands with bloody slaughter.

[2515] By chance then a fierce commander of the worldly court

[2571] had ordered the second offspring of Israel to go to the altar,

[2635] to make sacrifice to the dark idols, to become deserters of Christ.

[2704] A plague girt with steel pressed hard upon their free faith;

[2766] but that faith, brave, sought out of its own accord the rods,

[2829] the axes, the cloven claws, undaunted in the love of Christ.

[2891] The prison fetters their necks bound fast with hard rings;

[2951] the torturer plies his barbarous hands through all the forum;

[3014] truth is reckoned a crime, the faithful voice is punished.

[3074] Then valour, cut down by the sword, struck the grim ground,

[3135] and, cast upon the mournful pyres, drank in the flames with its mouth.

[3207] Then sweet it was to the just to be burned, sweet to endure the steel.

[3279] Here the dear hearts of two brothers grow warm together,

[3337] whom a faithful comradeship had joined through all their days.

[3401] They stand ready to bear whatever the last lot might bring,

[3462] whether the neck must be offered to the public axe

[3514] after the force of the cracking lash, after the racks of fire,

[3578] or the breast must be thrown to the leopards or the lions.

[3638] “Shall we, begotten for Christ, be dedicated to Mammon,

[3695] and, bearing the image of God, be slaves to the world?

[3751] Far be it that the heavenly fire should mingle itself with darkness.

[3821] Let it suffice that our life, seized under the first bond,

[3881] has paid every debt, having discharged what was Caesar’s;

[3940] it is time that what is God’s own be repaid to God.

[3993] Go, masters of the standards; and you, tribunes, stand aside.

[4056] Take away your golden collars, the rewards of the wounded!

[4116] Already from here the bright wages of the angels call us.

[4175] There Christ presides over cohorts robed in white,

[4227] and, reigning from his high throne, he condemns the infamous gods

[4294] and you, who fashion for yourselves laughable monsters as deities.”

[4363] As they speak these words the martyrs are crushed with a thousand torments;

[4440] a twisting rigour wraps both their hands in bonds,

[4492] and steel rings their chafed necks with heavy circles.

[4548] O the worn-out oblivion of a silent age!

[4590] These deeds are begrudged us, and their very fame is snuffed out.

[4657] For a blasphemous henchman once carried off the records,

[4715] lest the ages, instructed by enduring books,

[4761] should scatter through the ears of those to come

[4811] the order, the time, the manner of the passion handed down by sweet tongues.

[4889] Yet this alone the old silences withhold from us:

[4940] whether they fed their long hair under unbroken chains,

[4997] with what pain the torturer, with what parade, arrayed the men.

[5062] But this praise is not hidden, nor does it grow old with time:

[5126] that gifts, sent upward through the air, flew away,

[5179] gifts that, gleaming ahead, would show the way of heaven lying open.

[5249] Figuring the faith of one, a ring is borne upon a cloud;

[5307] the other gives, they say, a kerchief as pledge of his lips,

[5369] and these, snatched up by a breath from on high, enter the inmost light.

[5443] Through the axis of the flowing sky the flash of gold is hidden,

[5509] and the white of the cloth long flees the eye that follows it;

[5573] they are borne up even to the stars and are seen no more.

[5632] The crowd standing by saw this; the executioner himself saw it

[5696] and held back his hand, faltering, and grew pale with amazement;

[5762] yet still he carried through the stroke, lest the glory should be lost.

[5835] Do you believe now, you brutish heathenry of the Basques of old,

[5901] what sacred blood your cruel error sacrifices?

[5949] Do you believe that the souls of victims are carried up to God?

[6014] See how openly the fierce demons are tamed here,

[6064] who, seizing in wolfish fashion, devour men’s hearts,

[6119] strangle minds, and mingle themselves with the very senses.

[6180] Then, now full of his foe, the man is set there raving,

[6237] breathing out foaming spittle, twisting his savage eyes,

[6295] to be cleansed by an examination not of his own crimes.

[6352] You may hear—though no torturer stands by—the piteous wailings;

[6417] the body is torn under the scourge, yet no scourge is seen,

[6478] and the man himself, hung up, swells under hidden bonds.

[6536] By such means the martyrs’ power shakes the filthy robber,

[6596] this restrains him, racks, burns, this strikes him with chains;

[6661] the plunderer, tormented, strips himself from the marrow he had left.

[6732] He leaves the prey unharmed, he flees with dry jaws,

[6786] from the bottom of the nail to the hair he gives all back whole,

[6852] confessing that he himself burns; for he is a dweller in hell.

[6916] Why should I tell of bodies cleansed of long diseases and made white,

[6987] when a cold shuddering shakes the discoloured limbs,

[7041] here a swelling leaves the face, here the true colour returns?

[7105] This good the Saviour himself granted us to enjoy,

[7157] when he consecrated the limbs of the martyrs to our town,

[7216] which now keep safe the dwellers whom the Ebro washes.

[7272] Stand now and sing the hymn, you mothers, for your little ones recovered;

[7347] let the glad voice of wedded wives ring out for their husbands’ safety:

[7420] let this day be a feast for us, let it be a hallowed joy!
