WORK: Liber Peristephanon  (slug: aurelius-prudentius-clem-liber-peristephanon-42bcd7)

CHAPTER: 03-hymn-3



[0] 3. Hymn in Honour of the Passion of Eulalia, Most Blessed Martyr.

[67] Eulalia, noble in her lineage

[98] and nobler in her manner of death,

[134] honours her own Merida, the holy maiden—

[176] the city from whose breast she sprang—

[216] with her bones, and cherishes it in love.

[259] There is a place near the setting sun

[298] that bore this rare distinction,

[332] mighty in its city, rich in peoples,

[370] but mightier in the blood of martyrdom

[410] and powerful by the title of her virginity.

[455] Through three courses and nine

[487] she had four times reached three winters,

[530] when with a crackling pyre she terrified

[572] the trembling, harsh executioners,

[608] counting the torment sweet to her.

[644] She had given proof before

[672] that she stretched toward the Father’s throne

[719] and that her limbs were not dedicated to the marriage-bed:

[779] she had pushed away her very rattles,

[818] a little girl who knew not how to play;

[859] to scorn amber, to spurn roses,

[892] to reject golden necklaces,

[921] stern of face, modest in her step,

[957] and, with manners too tender,

[988] she rehearsed the grey hairs of the old.

[1030] But when the maddened plague

[1060] rouses itself against the Lord’s servants

[1103] and bloodily bids the worshippers of Christ

[1148] burn incense, to make smoke

[1177] of a beast’s liver to deadly gods,

[1213] the holy spirit of Eulalia

[1241] roared up, and fierce in temper

[1274] prepared to break the turbulent wars,

[1313] and, a woman, with breath untrained,

[1351] she provokes God to the arms of men.

[1389] But the pious care of her mother contrives

[1433] that the spirited maiden should hide at home,

[1480] concealed in the country and far from the city,

[1529] lest the wild girl, in love with death,

[1570] should rush to the price of her blood.

[1610] She, loathing to bear the relief of rest

[1652] with a degenerate delay,

[1678] by night, with no witness, stirs the doors

[1722] and, escaping, opens the enclosed bars;

[1763] then by trackless ways she takes her road.

[1807] She sets out with torn feet

[1836] through places rough with neglect and brambles,

[1885] attended by an angelic choir,

[1916] and though the bristling night is silent,

[1959] yet she has light for her guide.

[1993] So the noble throng of the fathers

[2029] had a pillar-bearing ray,

[2056] which, able to cut a path through the dark

[2100] night with a clear torch,

[2127] gave it as chaos passed away.

[2158] No otherwise the pious maiden, following

[2200] her road by night, deserved the day,

[2238] nor was she covered by the darkness,

[2276] while she fled the realms of Canopus

[2314] and made her way above the stars.

[2349] She, swift with sleepless step,

[2382] covers many miles before

[2408] the eastern region opens the sky;

[2443] at morning, proud, she comes to the tribunal,

[2490] stands amid the fasces

[2514] crying out: “I ask, what madness is it

[2554] to destroy souls headlong,

[2582] and, hearts wickedly lavish of themselves,

[2626] to throw them down on polished crags,

[2665] and to deny God the Father of all?

[2701] Do you seek, O pitiable band,

[2732] the race of Christ’s worshippers? Lo, here am I,

[2782] an enemy to the demonic rites;

[2814] I trample the idols beneath my feet,

[2852] and with heart and mouth I confess God.

[2893] Isis, Apollo, Venus are nothing,

[2927] and Maximian himself is nothing:

[2961] they nothing, because made by hand,

[2998] he, because he worships the works of hands,

[3043] both frivolous and both nothing.

[3077] Maximian, lord of wealth

[3103] and yet himself a client of stones,

[3140] let him prostitute and vow

[3168] his own head to his deities:

[3198] why does he shake noble breasts?

[3232] A good leader, a rare judge,

[3262] he feeds on innocent blood,

[3291] and, gaping after pious bodies,

[3324] he tears apart sober flesh,

[3353] and rejoices to torture faith.

[3385] Come then, torturer, burn, cut,

[3418] divide my limbs compacted of clay.

[3454] To loosen a fragile thing is easy:

[3490] the inner mind will not be pierced,

[3527] though pain harass it.”

[3552] Roused to fury by such words

[3582] the praetor says: “Seize the headstrong girl,

[3629] lictor, and overwhelm her with torments,

[3671] let her feel there are gods of her fathers

[3715] and that the prince’s rule is no light thing.

[3762] Yet how I would wish, before your death,

[3804] if it be possible, to call back

[3837] your wickedness, you grim little girl;

[3877] look back on what great joys you reap,

[3917] which a wedded honour brings you.

[3952] Your house, shaken with tears,

[3984] follows you, and the anxious

[4014] nobility of your line laments

[4045] that you die in the tender flower,

[4081] so near to dowries and the marriage-bed.

[4123] Does the golden pomp of the marriage-couch not move you,

[4181] nor the venerable affection of the old,

[4222] whom your reckless frailty wounds?

[4258] See, the services are ready

[4287] of a torturing death:

[4310] either you will be struck on the head with a sword,

[4363] or your limbs will be torn by beasts,

[4402] or, given to smoking torches

[4432] and to be wailed over piteously by your kin,

[4478] dissolved into ashes you will flow away.

[4520] What labour, I ask, is it to flee these?

[4562] If a little salt with your fingertips

[4601] and a little incense with your fingers

[4641] you would, kind maiden, deign to touch,

[4682] a grievous punishment will be far away.”

[4724] To this the martyr says nothing; but indeed

[4769] she roars, and into the tyrant’s eyes

[4808] she throws her spittle, then scatters

[4847] the images, and the meal set

[4877] on the censers she crushes with her foot.

[4920] Without delay, two executioners

[4953] tear her reed-slender breast,

[4984] and the claw strikes her maiden side

[5022] on both sides and cuts to the bone,

[5059] while Eulalia counts the marks.

[5092] “See, you write upon me, Lord;

[5124] how it delights to read these letters

[5163] that mark your trophies, Christ!

[5197] The very purple of the blood drawn forth

[5239] speaks the sacred name.”

[5265] These things, without weeping or groan,

[5306] glad and undaunted she sang;

[5336] dread pain is absent from her spirit,

[5375] and her limbs, painted with fresh gore,

[5416] a warm-flowing fount washes her skin.

[5455] Then came the final torture—

[5485] not a wounding laceration,

[5513] nor the skin ploughed to the rib-cage,

[5553] but a flame on every side, with torches,

[5595] rages against her flank and breast.

[5632] As her fragrant hair had flowed

[5665] over her throat, floating on her shoulders,

[5710] with which her bashful modesty

[5742] and a maiden’s honour might lie hidden,

[5783] the covering of her head set in its way,

[5825] the crackling flame flies into her face

[5866] and, fed through her hair, seizes

[5901] her head and rises above its crown;

[5938] the maiden, longing for a swift death,

[5978] reaches out and drinks the pyre with her mouth.

[6027] Then suddenly a dove darts forth,

[6062] whiter than snow, seen to leave

[6095] the martyr’s mouth and to follow the stars;

[6140] this was Eulalia’s spirit,

[6168] milk-white, swift, and harmless.

[6202] Her neck sinks as the soul departs

[6238] and the fiery pyre dies down;

[6269] peace is given to the lifeless limbs,

[6308] her breath rejoices and exults in the air

[6351] and, winged, seeks the lofty temples.

[6390] The attendant himself saw the bird

[6426] pass openly from the woman’s mouth,

[6463] and, stupefied and thunderstruck,

[6498] he leaps up and flees his own deeds,

[6536] and the lictor himself flees in terror.

[6577] Behold, the icy winter heaps up snow

[6615] and covers the whole forum,

[6644] and at the same time covers the limbs of Eulalia

[6694] lying under the frozen sky

[6722] like the linen of a little shroud.

[6758] Let the love of weeping men give way,

[6797] who are wont to celebrate the last rites,

[6840] let the tearful service give way:

[6875] the very elements, at God’s command,

[6913] bear your funeral, O maiden.

[6943] Now there is a place at Merida for her tomb,

[6989] a famous colony of Vettonia,

[7019] which the memorable river Ana

[7050] passes by, and, swift with its greening

[7091] current, washes the fair walls.

[7124] Here, where with clear marble

[7155] a kindly brightness lights the halls,

[7194] both the stranger and the native,

[7229] the venerable earth keeps the relics

[7267] and sacred ashes in its bosom.

[7299] Above, the gleaming roofs glow red

[7335] with little golden panels,

[7363] and cut stones variegate the floor,

[7400] so that you would think rosy meadows

[7438] were reddening with flowers of every kind.

[7482] Gather the purple violets

[7509] and reap the blood-red crocuses,

[7543] the genial winter does not lack these,

[7583] and the warming frost loosens the fields,

[7626] that it may heap the baskets with flowers.

[7670] Give these gifts from leafy boughs,

[7707] maiden and boy, give them.

[7735] But I, in the midst of the choir,

[7770] will bring garlands woven in dactylic measure,

[7818] cheap, withered, yet festal.

[7848] So it pleases to venerate her bones

[7885] and the altar set upon her bones:

[7920] she, placed beneath the feet of God,

[7958] looks upon these things, and, won by song,

[8002] cherishes her own peoples.
