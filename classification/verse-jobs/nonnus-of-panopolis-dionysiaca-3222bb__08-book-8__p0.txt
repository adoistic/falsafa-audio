[0] 8. The eighth holds the shifting-worded, savage jealousy of Hera, and the fiery marriage of Semele, and Zeus the slayer.

[122] So speaking, the god went to Olympus; but in the high-roofed

[184] hall his mind wandered near his bride,

[224] holding a sting for Thebes greater than for heaven; for to the son of Cronos

[302] heaven was lovely, the house of Semele, and about the wedding-chamber

[373] the handmaids of Cadmus were the swift-footed Seasons of Zeus.

[437] And with the marriage-droplets of the Zeus-sent wedding,

[495] pressed by its swelling, the womb of Semele heaved;

[548] and, as witness of the birth of garland-loving Dionysus,

[606] she delighted her heart with a wreath, and on her flower-tressed head

[677] she plaited the self-twining ivy of a maenad in her hair,

[736] like a prophetess of the Bassarids, and to brides to come,

[796] near their childbearing, she gave the late name of ivy.

[853] And bearing the heavy weight of the god-begotten birth,

[910] if ever some old shepherd made music on his pipe,

[961] hearing the song of neighbouring Echo, the lover of the fields,

[1026] in her single tunic she went out of the chamber with maenad rush;

[1093] if the sound of the double flute was heard, the wanderer of the hills,

[1165] leaping unsandalled from the high-roofed halls,

[1214] self-bidden she went out to the ridge of the lonely woodland;

[1277] if a cymbal clashed, she was stirred with a throb of her feet,

[1341] bounding her curving step beneath her slanting sandal;

[1397] and if she heard the long-horned bull lowing,

[1444] with a like imitation she bellowed the cry of an ox from her throat;

[1514] often, beneath the shepherd's ridge, with maenad voice

[1570] she sang a song with Pan, and Echo joined her cry,

[1622] and, answering the country sound of the horned flute,

[1677] she bent her step into the dance; and the unborn, wise child

[1739] with hidden leaps bounded along with the mother who bore him,

[1802] a flute-maddened imitation, and a self-taught song,

[1855] half-formed, he sounded forth, pouring an echo from within her womb.

[1925] So he was swelling with the weight of the womb that bore a son,

[1990] the herald of gladness, a thoughtful babe; and about the boy

[2052] the Seasons, handmaids of the son of Cronos, crowned heaven.

[2114] And Envy, gazing on the marriage-bed of high-ruling Zeus

[2172] and the birth-pangs of Semele, the god-begotten labour,

[2229] took on a jealousy of Bacchus while he was yet within the womb,

[2294] self-suffering, loveless, smitten by his own venom.

[2347] And in his crafty mind he devised a twisted plan,

[2398] bearing the lying form of an Ares opposed to him

[2448] in counterfeit armour, and, as if with a stream of blood,

[2507] with a poisonous flower he scored the back of an ox-hide

[2565] with a feigned droplet, and, as though from slaughtered men,

[2627] dipping his treacherous fingers in like-coloured red,

[2682] he reddened his hands with a bastard, ruddy gore;

[2733] and from his throat he belched a nine-thousand-fold din,

[2791] pouring from his fearful mouth a man-rending voice;

[2844] and with thieving whispers he startled Athena,

[2892] and goaded jealous Hera yet more to wrath;

[2936] he provoked them both, and with such words he reproached them:

[3000] Seek for yourself another, new bridegroom in heaven, Hera,

[3060] another, since Semele has snatched away yours, who for the grace of her bed

[3137] treads the wedded ground of seven-gated Thebes

[3185] and rejects the seven-zoned heaven; and in your stead

[3240] he delights to hold in his arms an earthly, pregnant bride.

[3301] Where has my mother's jealousy gone? Can it be that even

[3359] the wrath of Hera herself has been softened toward the marriage of Semele?

[3435] Where are your stings, your unsparing gadfly? No longer in the sea

[3503] is the heifer driven, sea-frightened? No longer does the herdsman

[3570] Argus, with his sleepless, far-scattered eyes,

[3618] keep watch over the newer bed of the marriage-thieving son of Cronos?

[3689] But what is this Olympian crowd to me? Going down to earth

[3749] I will leave my father's heaven, and dwelling in our own

[3807] Thrace I shall not see the sorrows of my grieving mother,

[3866] nor Zeus the marriage-thief; and if ever he comes

[3917] to our land, desiring a Bistonian maiden,

[3960] he shall learn what Ares is when he is angered; for, bearing our own

[4030] death-bringing spear, the slayer of the Titans,

[4079] I will drive the woman-mad son of Cronos out of Thrace;

[4136] and taking as my pretext that he drags a maiden to his bed,

[4197] self-bidden I shall be the avenger of my own marriage,

[4253] because, consorting with earth-born weddings,

[4300] he has filled the dappled heaven with his loves.

[4350] May heaven be gracious, the house of mortals; do I tread the pole of stars?

[4427] Callisto wheels along Olympus, where the circle shines

[4483] of the high-crested Bear, named for the Arcadian.

[4534] I hate the course of the seven-pathed Pleiad; for in Olympus

[4596] Electra troubles me, flashing together with Selene.

[4649] Why are you now at ease? You afflicted Apollo, the son of Leto,

[4714] yet within the womb, and you do not stir against Dionysus?

[4774] When the Trito-born was born, Hephaestus, the labour-easer,

[4835] self-bearing Zeus shall bring to birth the son of a bastard wife,

[4902] bringing to labour another, higher birth in his male thigh,

[4963] and there is no more need of your axe. Yield, Athena,

[5018] cease crying out over the birth-arch of the head of Zeus,

[5077] because Dionysus disgraces the wise birth-pang of your

[5133] birth-perfecting head, in that, sprung from an earthly stock,

[5196] he will be self-born, an Olympian, like Athena,

[5245] hiding the boast of motherless Pallas. Nay, I myself

[5299] am ashamed far more, whenever some mortal says:

[5348] Zeus gave battle to Ares and gladness to Dionysus.

[5400] But leaving the pole of the son of Cronos to his bastard children,

[5468] I will come down from heaven, a wanderer; and let the water-frozen

[5536] Ister receive its sceptred lord roaming about,

[5584] before I see Ganymede here, the cup-bearer of Zeus,

[5637] the fair-haired herdsman, the citizen of Olympus after Pergamon,

[5703] handing the untouched cup of heavenly Hebe,

[5748] before I see Semele and Bacchus dwellers on Olympus,

[5802] and the star-wandering crown of earthly Ariadne

[5851] running with the Sun, the fellow-traveller of the dawn.

[5908] There I will stay, that I may not see the Sea-Monster, nor the sickle of Perseus,

[5991] nor the figure of Andromeda, nor the eye of the Gorgon Medusa,

[6055] which the son of Cronos may hereafter set in Olympus.

[6110] He spoke, and confounded the mind of self-born Athena,

[6166] and raised yet higher the wrath of heavy-jealous Hera.

[6222] And Envy darted swiftly off, and shaking his crooked knees

[6282] went by slanting paths through the air; and in his

[6334] eyes and his mind he rushed away, like smoke,

[6381] arming his telchin-mind for guile, for wickedness.

[6497] but, darting along on her storm-swift sandal,

[6544] she ran through the dappled heaven adorned with bright stars,

[6657] seeking crafty Deceit, in case she might find her somewhere.

[6719] But when, high above the Dictaean rock of the Corybants,

[6879] for she always lingered by the false tomb of Zeus,

[6931] taking delight in the Cretans, since they were deceivers.

[6990] And about her loins flowed the Cydonian girdle,

[7088] in it is crafty thievery, in it dalliance,

[7132] cajolery, in it manifold deceits, and in it also

[7238] And to crafty Deceit, with crafty speech, Hera

[7286] of cunning counsel made soft entreaty, warding off her consort:

[7351] Hail, goddess crafty-counselled, guile-wandering; not even

[7411] wheedling Hermes passes you by with his thieving whispers;

[7471] give me also the all-shifting girdle, which once Rheia

[7527] bound about her loins, until she deceived her consort.

[7583] I do not bear a rocky form against the son of Cronos,

[7638] nor do I beguile a consort with a deceitful stone,

[14178] Thunderbolts are the bride-attendants of Hera and Semele.

[14237] Jealous though she is, the wife of Zeus will not be vexed at you;

[14409] horned Zeus lifted up; and on the love-stricken bull's

[14465] topmost hoof she ran dry over the water,

[14507] and so great a one was the boat of Eros. Ah, great marvel,

[14717] in a rich droplet of woman-maddened snow;

[14760] yet the most blessed bride did not ask for golden gifts,

[14818] for she had her husband as the whole love-gift of Eros. But let there be

[14892] some silence between us, lest your sire Cadmus hear.

[14946] So speaking she left the house, and the bride still grieving,

[15009] holding the jealousy of Hera, of unmatched weddings,

[15063] blaming the son of Cronos; and by the back-returning path

[15312] Thunder, has my cloud-gathering Zeus left you too?

[15364] Who has snatched you again, and stripped you of your bearer?

[15426] Thunder, you have been despoiled—Typhoeus is not the cause—

[15487] sharing the sufferings of Hera, my comforter; for rainy Zeus,

[15550] the bride-tender, rejects us both.

[15586] No longer is the earth dusted with snows, and, the moist-poured

[15651] rain failing, the drought feeds upon the furrow of the ploughland,

[15763] Zeus is called cloudless instead of dark-clouding.

[15815] Lightnings, break a fiery voice against the son of Cronos,

[15875] against woman-mad Zeus; friends, thunderbolts, cry out.

[16389] wishing for the form of Hera's fire-crowned bed:

[16439] By Danae I beseech you, of the wealth-streaming weddings,

[16558] to call you the consort of Semele, whom I saw as in a dream.
