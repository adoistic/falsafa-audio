[0] 1. Satire I Must I be always only a listener? Never strike back,

[66] so often racked by hoarse Cordus and his Theseid?

[117] Shall this one read me out his comedies unpunished,

[170] that one his elegies? Shall some monstrous Telephus

[223] burn up my whole day unavenged, or an Orestes

[270] written to the margin's edge and onto the back, still not finished?

[339] No man knows his own house better than I know

[386] the grove of Mars and Vulcan's cave hard by the Aeolian crags.

[450] What the winds are at, what shades Aeacus

[493] is racking, from what spot another sneaks off the gold

[549] of the stolen fleece, how huge the ash-trees Monychus hurls—

[611] Fronto's plane-trees and battered marble shriek it

[663] forever, his columns cracked by the relentless reciter.

[720] You may expect the same from poet greatest and least.

[775] I too have pulled my hand back from the schoolmaster's cane, I too

[843] have counseled Sulla to sleep soundly as a private man;

[900] it is foolish mercy, when you meet so many bards

[950] on every side, to spare the paper that is doomed to perish.

[1011] Yet why I would rather run my course over this same field

[1070] down which Aurunca's great foster-son drove his horses,

[1127] I will tell you, if you have leisure and will calmly hear my reason.

[1197] When a soft eunuch takes a wife, when Mevia spears

[1249] a Tuscan boar and grips the hunting-spear with breast laid bare,

[1315] when one man alone challenges all the patricians in wealth—

[1376] the man whose razor rasped my stiff young beard;

[1426] when a piece of the Nile's rabble, when Canopus' homebred slave

[1491] Crispinus hitches up the Tyrian cloak on his shoulder

[1546] and airs his summer gold on sweating fingers,

[1593] unable to bear the weight of a heavier gem—

[1638] it is hard not to write satire. For who is so patient

[1693] of this unjust city, so iron-hard, that he can hold himself in,

[1758] when up comes the brand-new litter of the pleader Matho,

[1816] filled with the man himself; behind him the informer on his great friend,

[1891] soon to snatch what survives of the gnawed nobility,

[1945] the man whom Massa fears, whom Carus soothes with a bribe,

[2005] and to whom trembling Latinus sends Thymele in secret?

[2061] When men shove you aside who earn legacies

[2105] by their nights' work, and are raised to heaven by the surest road

[2173] now to high advancement—an old rich woman's lust?

[2224] Proculeius gets a twelfth, but Gillo eleven-twelfths,

[2279] each heir's share matched to the measure of his loins.

[2335] Let him take his wage of blood, by all means, and grow

[2391] as pale as the man who has trodden a snake with naked heel,

[2452] or the orator about to speak at the Lyons altar.

[2502] Why should I tell how my dry liver blazes with rage,

[2556] when this plunderer crowds the people with his troops of attendants—

[2626] he who robbed his ward and drove the boy to the streets, and that one

[2697] condemned by a verdict worth nothing? For what is disgrace, with the cash kept safe?

[2783] Marius, an exile, drinks from the eighth hour and enjoys the angry

[2851] gods, while you, victorious province, weep.

[2896] Shall I not think these things worthy of the Venusine lamp?

[2957] Shall I not have at them? But why rather Hercules' labors,

[3017] or Diomedes', or the bellowing of the labyrinth,

[3067] and the sea struck by the boy and the craftsman who flew,

[3126] when the pimp takes the adulterer's goods, if the wife

[3182] has no right to inherit—skilled to study the ceiling,

[3237] skilled to snore into his cup with wakeful nose?

[3287] When a man thinks he may rightly hope to command a cohort

[3346] who has squandered his fortune on stables and lost all

[3402] his ancestors' wealth, while he flies down the Flaminian Way

[3464] at full speed, a boy Automedon? For he held the reins himself,

[3528] when he showed off before his cloaked mistress.

[3577] Is one not tempted to fill broad tablets in the middle

[3633] of the crossroads, when there is carried by, on six men's necks now,

[3703] open on this side and that, all but reclining bare in his chair,

[3769] and looking much like the lolling Maecenas,

[3814] a forger who has made himself sleek and rich

[3860] by a scrap of paper and a moistened seal?

[3903] Up comes a powerful matron who, about to pass

[3950] her husband soft Calenian wine, mixes in a toad,

[4000] and—a better Locusta—teaches her green neighbors

[4050] to carry out their blackened husbands through gossip and the crowd.

[4119] Dare something worthy of cramped Gyara and the cell,

[4173] if you would be anything; honesty is praised and freezes.

[4232] To their crimes men owe their gardens, mansions, tables,

[4290] old silver and the goat that stands out from the cup.

[4345] Who can sleep when the corrupter of the greedy daughter-in-law,

[4410] when shameless brides and the boy-adulterer still in his bordered gown, are about?

[4494] If nature denies it, indignation makes the verse,

[4545] such as it can—the sort that I, or Cluvienus, write.

[4599] Ever since Deucalion, when the clouds raised the sea,

[4654] climbed the mountain in his ship and asked the oracle for an answer,

[4724] and little by little the soft stones grew warm with life,

[4783] and Pyrrha showed naked girls to the men,

[4826] whatever men do—prayer, fear, rage, pleasure,

[4873] joy, bustling about—is the medley of our little book.

[4928] And when was there a richer crop of vices? When did

[4981] the lap of avarice gape wider? When did the dice-box

[5035] hold such spirits? For men do not go to the hazard of the table

[5100] with purse in pocket, but play with the strongbox set down beside them.

[5173] What battles you will see there, with the steward for armor-bearer!

[5242] Is it plain madness to lose a hundred thousand sesterces

[5300] and not give a tunic to a shivering slave?

[5344] Which of our grandfathers built so many villas, which one dined

[5409] on seven courses by himself, in private? Now a little dole

[5469] sits on the outer threshold, to be snatched by the toga-clad mob;

[5536] yet the giver first inspects each face, and trembles lest

[5595] you come as an impostor and claim under a false name:

[5650] once recognized, you will get it. He bids the crier summon

[5710] even the true-born Trojans, for they too besiege the threshold

[5774] along with us. "Give to the praetor, then give to the tribune."

[5839] But a freedman comes first. "I was here first," he says, "I am present.

[5912] Why should I fear or hesitate to defend my place, though

[5970] I was born by the Euphrates—the soft holes in my ears

[6025] would prove it, though I deny it myself? But my five shops

[6085] bring in four hundred thousand. What does the broad purple confer

[6152] worth wishing for, if a Corvinus guards in the Laurentine fields

[6218] his hired sheep, while I own more

[6253] than Pallas and the Licini?" Let the tribunes wait, then;

[6312] let riches win; let the man not yield his sacred office

[6369] who came lately to this city with whitened feet,

[6419] since among us the most sacred majesty is that of riches—

[6478] though, deadly Money, you do not yet dwell in a temple,

[6535] we have raised no altars to coin

[6569] as we worship Peace and Faith, Victory, Virtue,

[6618] and Concord, who chatters when men greet her nest.

[6670] But when the highest magistrate reckons up at the year's end

[6732] what the dole brings in, how much it adds to his accounts,

[6792] what shall the clients do, whose toga, whose shoes come from this,

[6860] and whose bread and hearth-smoke at home? The litters press in thick

[6930] after the hundred farthings, and the wife follows her husband,

[6994] sick or pregnant, and is carried round in the throng.

[7049] This one, a practiced cheat by a familiar trick, begs for an absent wife,

[7124] displaying an empty, shut sedan in her stead:

[7171] "It is my Galla," he says, "let us through quickly, you delay us—

[7238] show your head, Galla. No, do not trouble her, she is resting."

[7303] The day itself is marked off in a fine order of affairs:

[7361] first the dole, then the forum and Apollo learned in law,

[7420] and the triumphal statues, among which some Egyptian—

[7475] I know not who—an Arabarch, has dared to set his titles,

[7533] at whose effigy it is allowed to do worse than make water.

[7593] The old and weary clients leave the porches

[7638] and lay down their hopes, though a man's longest-lived

[7694] hope is dinner; the poor wretches must buy their cabbage and their fire.

[7768] Meanwhile their king will devour the best of woods and waters,

[7832] and lie alone at his empty couches.

[7869] For from so many fine and broad and ancient

[7914] tables they eat up whole fortunes at one sitting.

[7965] There will soon be no parasites at all. But who could endure

[8027] such squalid luxury? How great the gullet that serves up to itself

[8095] whole boars, a beast born to be a feast!

[8137] Yet the punishment is at hand, when you peel off your clothes

[8200] bloated, and carry an undigested peacock into the baths;

[8258] hence sudden deaths and old age cut off without a will,

[8315] and the new tale runs—and no one grieves—through all the dinners:

[8382] the funeral is led out to applauding, angry friends.

[8436] There will be nothing left for our descendants to add

[8491] to our morals; the men to come will do and crave the same.

[8551] Every vice has reached its sheer cliff-edge: spread your sails,

[8616] throw open all your canvas. Here perhaps you say: "Where

[8674] is genius to match the matter? Where is that frankness

[8730] of the ancients in writing whatever the burning heart

[8785] was pleased to write? 'Whose name dare I not speak?

[8838] What does it matter whether Mucius pardons my words or not?'"

[8901] Name Tigellinus, and you will blaze in the same torch

[8956] in which they stand and burn who smoke with throats pierced through,

[9026] and you trace a broad furrow through the middle of the sand.

[9088] So the man who gave three uncles their aconite is to be borne aloft

[9157] on cushioned feathers, and from there look down on us?

[9213] "When he comes toward you, put a finger to your lip:

[9267] he will be an informer who only says 'That's the man.'

[9323] You may safely set Aeneas against the fierce Rutulian,

[9379] it troubles no one that Achilles was struck down,

[9430] or Hylas, much sought after, who followed his pitcher.

[9486] But whenever Lucilius, ablaze as with drawn sword,

[9538] roars out, the listener reddens, whose mind is cold

[9591] with crime, and his heart sweats with secret guilt:

[9644] hence anger and tears. So turn these things over

[9694] in your mind before the trumpets sound; once helmeted, it is too late

[9765] to repent the war." I will try what is allowed against those

[9827] whose ashes are covered by the Flaminian Way and the Latin.

[9888] 2. Satire II I long to flee beyond the Sarmatians and the icy

[9951] Ocean, whenever men make bold to speak of morals

[10001] who ape the Curii and live like Bacchanals—

[10046] unlearned, in the first place, though you find every shelf

[10106] stuffed with plaster Chrysippuses; for the most perfect of them

[10171] is whoever buys some likeness of Aristotle or Pittacus

[10227] and bids his cases guard the originals of the Cleanthes.

[10285] There is no trusting the face; for what street does not abound

[10349] with grim debauchees? You chastise foul deeds, when you are

[10410] the most notorious ditch among the Socratic deviants?

[10465] Shaggy limbs, indeed, and stiff bristles along the arms

[10522] promise a fierce spirit; but the surgeon laughs as he cuts

[10582] the swollen piles on your smooth backside.

[10626] They have little to say, and a vast lust for silence,

[10681] and hair shorter than their eyebrows. Truer therefore,

[10737] and more honest, is Peribomius; him I charge to the fates,

[10797] who confesses his disease by his face and his gait.

[10850] Their plain folly is pitiable; their very frenzy

[10900] earns them pardon. But worse are those who attack such things

[10963] in the words of a Hercules, and after preaching virtue

[11019] waggle the rump. "Am I to respect you when you wriggle, Sextus?"

[11085] asks infamous Varillus. "How am I worse than you?

[11136] Let the straight man mock the bandy-legged, the white man the Ethiopian.

[11210] Who could endure the Gracchi complaining of sedition?

[11265] Who would not confound heaven with earth and sea with sky,

[11325] if Verres disliked a thief, Milo a murderer,

[11371] Clodius accused adulterers, Catiline Cethegus,

[11419] if the three pupils railed against Sulla's proscription-roll?

[11482] Such was that adulterer lately defiled by a union

[11533] worthy of tragedy, who was then reviving the bitter laws

[11591] dreaded by all, and even by Venus and Mars themselves,

[11647] while Julia, fruitful, emptied her womb of so many

[11699] abortions, pouring out lumps that resembled her uncle.

[11755] Is it not then by right and merit that the lowest vices

[11812] despise the false Scauri, and bite back when they are chastised?

[11878] One of them Laronia could not stand—a grim fellow

[11929] shouting again and again, "Where now, Julian Law? Are you asleep?"

[11997] And so with a smile she said: "Happy times, that set

[12051] you up against our morals! Now let Rome have shame:

[12104] a third Cato has fallen from the sky. But still, where

[12160] do you buy these things—this balsam that breathes from your hairy

[12227] neck? Don't be ashamed to point out the master of the shop.

[12288] But if laws and rights are to be vexed, the Scantinian

[12344] should be cited before all: look first at the men

[12395] and examine them; they do more, but their numbers

[12446] defend them, and their close-locked phalanx of shields.

[12503] There is great concord among the soft ones; you will find no

[12565] example in our sex so detestable.

[12600] Media does not lick Cluvia, nor Flora Catulla:

[12648] it is Hispo who submits to young men, and pales with the double disease.

[12722] Do we plead cases, do we know the civil law,

[12768] or stir your courts with any uproar?

[12806] A few of us wrestle, a few eat athletes' diet:

[12854] you spin the wool and bring back in baskets the finished

[12912] fleeces, you twist the spindle pregnant with fine thread

[12970] better than Penelope, more deftly than Arachne,

[13019] such work as the bristly slave-mistress does, perched on her block.

[13088] It is well known why Hister filled his tablets to his freedman

[13152] alone, why in his lifetime he gave the young wife so much.

[13212] She will be rich who sleeps a third in the great bed.

[13267] So marry and keep silent: secrets buy you jewels.

[13318] After all this, is the grim sentence passed against us?

[13375] The censorship pardons the crows and harasses the doves."

[13434] The Stoic spawn fled in a panic from her, who sang

[13486] what was true and plain; for what false thing did Laronia say? But what

[13559] will others not do, when you put on gauze, Creticus,

[13613] and harangue the marveling people in this dress

[13662] against the Proculae and Pollittae? "Fabulla is an adulteress,

[13726] condemn her, if you like; condemn Carfinia too: but even

[13784] condemned she would not wear such a gown." "But it is July, I am scorching,

[13861] I sweat." Then plead naked: madness is less disgraceful.

[13919] Look at the garb in which you propose laws and rights

[13974] to a people but now victorious with their wounds still raw,

[14035] and to that mountain rabble who have laid down their plows.

[14096] What would you not cry out, if you saw such stuff

[14147] on the body of a judge? I ask whether gauze becomes a witness.

[14211] You, the keen and untamed master of liberty,

[14257] Creticus, are see-through. This taint a plague has spread,

[14317] and will spread to more, just as in the fields a whole herd

[14378] falls from one pig's scab and mange,

[14416] and one grape draws its livid color from another.

[14467] Some day you will dare something fouler than this dress;

[14525] no one ever became the vilest of men at a stroke. Little by little

[14593] those will take you in who at home put on long fillets

[14649] over their brows, and have hung necklaces all round their throats,

[14717] and appease the Good Goddess with the paunch of a young sow

[14778] and a great mixing-bowl; but by a sinister reversal

[14831] a woman is driven far off and may not cross the threshold:

[14891] the goddess' altar is open to males alone. "Away, profane women!"

[14958] is the cry—no flute-girl moans here on her horn.

[15008] Such were the rites that the Baptae, wont to wear out

[15063] Athenian Cotytto, kept by torchlight in secret.

[15112] One draws out his eyebrow, stained with moist soot,

[15165] with a slanting needle, and paints his fluttering eyes,

[15222] lifting them; another drinks from a glass Priapus,

[15274] and binds his hair in a golden net,

[15311] clothed in sky-blue checks or smooth pale-green,

[15361] while the slave swears by Juno after his master's fashion.

[15421] That one holds a mirror, the gear of the pathic Otho,

[15476] "the spoil of Auruncan Actor," in which he viewed himself

[15535] in armor, when he was already ordering the standards raised—

[15597] a thing to be recorded in fresh annals and recent

[15648] history: a mirror among the baggage of a civil war!

[15701] Of course it takes a supreme general to kill Galba

[15753] and keep his skin smooth; the steadiness of a supreme citizen

[15816] to grasp at the spoils of the Palace on the fields of Bebriacum,

[15882] and to smear his face with the dough spread by his fingers—

[15943] what neither quivered Semiramis did in her Assyrian realm,

[16003] nor mournful Cleopatra on her Actian ship.

[16047] Here is no shame in their words, no reverence for the table;

[16109] here is the foul license of Cybele and the cracked

[16161] falsetto voice; an old fanatic with white hair,

[16210] the priest of the rites, a rare and memorable example

[16265] of a great gullet, a master worth hiring.

[16308] Yet what do they wait for? It were high time for them now,

[16368] Phrygian-fashion, to cut off the superfluous flesh with the knife.

[16436] Gracchus gave four hundred thousand as a dowry

[16484] to a horn-blower—or had this one sounded the straight trumpet?

[16548] The contract was signed, "Good luck!" was cried, a huge

[16605] dinner sat down, the new bride lay in her husband's lap.

[16663] O nobles, do we need a censor or a soothsayer?

[16711] Would you, I suppose, shudder and reckon it a greater portent,

[16775] if a woman gave birth to a calf, or a cow to a lamb?

[16829] He puts on the trimmed dress and the long robes and the bridal veil,

[16899] he who, bearing the sacred objects swaying on the mystic strap,

[16964] sweated under the sacred shields. O father of the city,

[17021] whence came such wickedness upon the Latin shepherds? Whence

[17083] did this nettle sting your grandsons, Gradivus?

[17132] See—a man renowned for birth and wealth is handed over to a man

[17197] in marriage, and you neither shake your helmet nor strike the earth

[17266] with your spear, nor complain to your father? Go then, and quit

[17331] the acres of the stern Campus that you neglect. "A duty tomorrow

[17397] at sunrise must be done by me in Quirinus' valley."

[17450] What is the duty's occasion? "Why ask? A friend is taking

[17509] a husband, and has invited only a few." Just let us live, and

[17572] these things will happen, will happen openly, will crave to be entered

[17644] in the records. Meanwhile a great torment grips these brides:

[17707] that they cannot bear children, and keep their husbands by childbirth.

[17779] But it is better that nature grants the mind

[17825] no power over the body: barren they die, and for them

[17880] it is no use that swollen Lyde keeps her drugs in a box,

[17938] nor any use to offer their palms to the nimble Lupercus.

[17996] Yet Gracchus outdid even this monster, in a tunic,

[18048] with his trident, and ranged in flight across the middle of the sand,

[18119] a gladiator more nobly born than the Capitolini and the Marcelli,

[18186] than the descendants of Catulus and Paulus and the Fabii,

[18245] than all the spectators on the podium—grant that you place there

[18311] the very man at whose show he then cast the net.

[18361] That there are any shades and underground realms,

[18412] a Cocytus, and black frogs in the Stygian pool,

[18461] and that so many thousands cross the ford in a single skiff—

[18523] not even boys believe, except those too young to bathe for a coin.

[18591] But suppose them true: what does Curius feel, and both

[18647] the Scipios, what Fabricius and the shade of Camillus,

[18703] what the legion of the Cremera and the youth consumed at Cannae,

[18769] so many souls of so many wars, whenever such

[18815] a ghost comes to them from here? They would long to be cleansed,

[18881] if there were sulphur to be had with torches, and a moist laurel-branch.

[18955] There, alas, we wretches are paraded. Our arms indeed

[19010] we have pushed beyond the shores of Ireland and the lately taken

[19076] Orkneys, and the Britons content with their brief night;

[19134] but the things now done in the city of the conquering people,

[19197] those whom we conquered do not do. And yet one

[19245] Armenian, Zalaces, softer than all the youths,

[19293] is said to have yielded himself to a passionate tribune.

[19351] See what intercourse does: he had come as a hostage—

[19405] here they are made men. For if a longer stay has indulged

[19464] the boys with the city, they will never lack a lover.

[19519] Off go the trousers, the knives, the bridle, the whip:

[19575] so they carry back to Artaxata the manners of Rome.

[19628] 3. Satire III Though shaken by my old friend's departure,

[19687] I yet approve that he resolves to fix his home in empty Cumae

[19750] and present one citizen to the Sibyl.

[19789] It is the gateway to Baiae and a pleasant shore of agreeable

[19851] retreat; I myself prefer even Prochyta to the Subura.

[19906] For what so wretched, so lonely a thing have we seen, that you would not

[19980] think it worse to dread the constant fires, the everlasting

[20041] collapse of houses, and the thousand perils of the cruel

[20099] city, and the poets reciting in the month of August?

[20153] But while his whole household was being packed into a single cart,

[20221] he halted at the old arches and the dripping Capena gate,

[20280] here, where Numa used to meet his nightly mistress.

[20333] Now the grove of the sacred spring and its shrines are leased out

[20400] to Jews, whose furniture is a basket and some hay

[20451] (for every tree is ordered to pay the people its rent,

[20507] and the wood, with the Muses cast out, goes begging).

[20562] We go down into the vale of Egeria and the caves

[20612] unlike the real ones—how much nearer would the spirit

[20667] of the waters be, if grass enclosed the streams

[20716] with a green margin, and no marble violated the native rock.

[20778] Here then Umbricius said: "Since for honest skills

[20830] there is no place in the city, no reward for labor,

[20883] since my estate is less today than it was yesterday, and tomorrow

[20950] the same will wear away yet a little more, I am minded to go

[21012] there where Daedalus put off his weary wings,

[21059] while my gray hair is new, while old age is fresh and upright,

[21123] while something is left for Lachesis to spin, and I carry myself

[21189] on my own feet, with no staff propping my right hand.

[21244] Let us yield our native land. Let Artorius live there

[21299] and Catulus, let those stay who turn black into white,

[21355] for whom it is easy to take contracts for a temple, rivers, harbors,

[21425] draining a flood, carrying a corpse to the pyre,

[21475] and to offer a head for sale under the master's spear.

[21531] Once these men were horn-blowers, the perpetual companions

[21591] of the municipal arena, known by their puffed cheeks through the towns;

[21664] now they put on shows, and when the mob with thumb turned

[21723] bids it, they kill to please the crowd; then, back again,

[21782] they take contracts for public privies—and why not everything,

[21846] since they are the kind whom Fortune, whenever she chooses to play,

[21915] lifts from the lowest dregs to the highest pinnacles of things?

[21980] What should I do at Rome? I cannot lie: a book,

[22029] if it is bad, I cannot praise and beg a copy of; the motions

[22091] of the stars I do not know; to promise a man his father's

[22150] funeral I neither wish nor am able; the entrails of frogs

[22209] I have never examined; what the adulterer sends to a bride,

[22270] what he charges, others know how to carry; with me as helper

[22332] no man will be a thief, and so I go forth no one's companion,

[22395] like a cripple, a body useless with a dead right hand.

[22451] Who is loved now, except an accomplice, and one whose seething

[22515] mind boils with secrets that must forever be kept silent?

[22574] He thinks he owes you nothing, will never give you anything,

[22636] who has made you sharer of an honest secret;

[22682] dear to Verres will be the man who can accuse Verres

[22736] whenever he wishes. Let not all the sand of shaded Tagus

[22794] and the gold it rolls into the sea be worth so much to you,

[22855] that you should lose your sleep and take rewards that must

[22915] sadly be laid down, and forever be feared by a great friend.

[22977] What race is now most acceptable to our rich men,

[23028] and whom above all I would flee, I will hasten to confess,

[23088] nor will shame stand in my way. I cannot bear, Romans,

[23144] a Greek Rome; and yet how small a fraction of the dregs is Achaean?

[23213] Long since the Syrian Orontes has flowed into the Tiber,

[23271] and brought with it its language and its morals, and its strings

[23337] slanting with the flute, and its native timbrels too,

[23392] and the girls bidden to stand for hire by the circus.

[23447] Off with you, all who fancy a barbarian whore in a painted turban!

[23515] Your rustic, Quirinus, puts on Greek dinner-slippers,

[23570] and wears Greek prize-medals on his oil-smeared neck.

[23625] This man from high Sicyon, that one having left Amydon,

[23682] this from Andros, that from Samos, this from Tralles or Alabanda,

[24029] grammarian, rhetor, geometer, painter, masseur,

[24302] Am I not to flee these men's purple? Shall that fellow

[24358] sign before me, and recline propped on a better couch,

[24414] borne to Rome by the wind that brings plums and figs?

[24469] Does it count for nothing, then, that my infancy drank in

[24528] the Aventine air, and was nourished on the Sabine berry?

[24586] What of this—that this race, most expert in flattery, praises

[24649] the talk of the ignorant, the face of an ugly friend,

[24704] and likens a weak man's long neck to the shoulders

[24756] of Hercules holding Antaeus high off the earth,

[24805] and admires a thin voice, no worse than which sounds

[24956] are believed; for is any actor better, when he plays Thais,

[25017] or the wife, or Doris ungirded by any cloak?

[25278] nor Demetrius with soft Haemus: theirs is a nation of actors.

[26171] to their schools, and hear the crime of a graver gown.

[26227] A Stoic, an old man, an informer, killed his friend Barea,

[26287] his own pupil, reared on that bank

[26323] to which the wing of the Gorgon's horse glided down.

[26377] There is no place here for any Roman, where there reigns

[26435] some Protogenes or Diphilus or Hermarchus,

[26479] who by his race's vice never shares a friend,

[26526] but keeps him to himself; for once he has dripped into the willing ear

[26650] I am thrust from the door, and the long years

[26697] of my service are wasted; nowhere is the loss of a client counted less.

[26770] Besides—not to flatter ourselves—what duty, or what

[26823] merit of a poor man, is there here, if he troubles to run

[26882] in his toga by night, when the praetor drives on his lictor

[26943] and bids him hurry headlong to greet the long-wakeful childless ladies,

[27016] lest his colleague salute Albina and Modia first?

[27067] Here the freeborn son of a citizen yields the wall

[27119] to a rich man's slave; for one of these gives Calvina or Catiena

[27231] to writhe upon her once or twice; but you,

[27275] when the face of a well-dressed harlot pleases you, hang back

[27338] and hesitate to hand Chione down from her lofty chair.

[27445] of the Idaean deity; let Numa come forward, or the man

[27501] who saved trembling Minerva from her burning shrine:

[27616] comes last of all. "How many slaves does he feed? How many acres

[27682] of land does he own? On how big and how full a platter does he dine?"

[27753] As much money as a man keeps in his strongbox,

[27801] so much credit he has. Though you swear by the altars

[27856] of Samothrace and our own, the poor man is believed to scorn

[28105] if his toga is a little dirty and one shoe gapes

[30486] or at Volsinii nestled among the wooded ridges, or at

[32651] from which you might give a feast to a hundred Pythagoreans.

[35024] what a height it is to the lofty roofs from which a potsherd

[35086] strikes your brain, how often cracked and chipped vessels

[35145] fall from the windows, with what weight they mark and gash

[35205] the pavement they strike. You might be thought careless

[35262] and heedless of sudden mishap, if you go out to dinner

[35318] without making your will: there are as many fates

[35369] as there are wakeful open windows on that night as you pass by.

[35434] So pray, and carry with you the pitiable wish

[35481] that they may be content to pour down on you only their broad basins.

[35614] pays the penalty, and passes a night like that of Achilles

[35674] mourning his friend: he lies face down, then soon on his back;

[35738] a brawl gives him sleep. But however reckless with his years

[35800] and hot with wine, he steers clear of the man whom the scarlet cloak

[35870] bids him avoid, and the very long train of attendants,

[35926] and besides the many torches and the lamp of bronze.

[35980] But me, whom the moon is wont to escort, or the brief light

[36086] he scorns. Learn the prelude to the wretched brawl,

[36139] if it is a brawl, where you strike and I only take the blows.

[36254] for what can you do, when a madman drives you on, and the same man

[36396] whose beans are you bloated? What cobbler ate sliced leek with you,

[36465] and the lips of a boiled wether?

[36545] Tell me where you doss—in what synagogue shall I look for you?"

[36667] it is all the same: they beat you just as much, and then in anger

[36797] beaten, he begs, and pummeled with fists, he prays

[36849] that he may be let go home with a few of his teeth.

[36902] Nor is this all you have to fear; for there will be no lack

[36963] of one to rob you when the houses are shut, after every

[37020] chain-bound, bolted shop has fallen silent everywhere.

[37076] Sometimes too a footpad does his work suddenly with the knife;

[37140] whenever the Pomptine marsh and the Gallinarian pine

[37194] are kept safe by an armed guard,

[37228] from there they all run here as if to a game-preserve.

[37284] What furnace, what anvil, is not heavy with chains?

[37337] The largest amount of iron goes into fetters, so that you may fear

[37405] the plowshare will run short, that hoes and mattocks will fail.

[37470] Happy, you would call the forefathers of our forefathers, happy

[37625] To these I could add many more reasons besides;

[37674] but the beasts are calling, and the sun sinks, I must go;

[37847] Rome restores you, hastening for refreshment, to your own Aquinum,

[38088] 4. Satire IV Here is Crispinus again, and I must often call him

[38153] to play his part—a monster redeemed by no virtue

[38203] from his vices, a sickly voluptuary strong only in lust,

[38261] an adulterer who spurns none but widows.

[38303] What does it matter, then, how great the colonnades

[38356] in which he wearies his horses, in how vast a shade of woods

[38418] he is carried, how many acres he has bought near the forum, what houses?

[38492] the incestuous man, with whom but lately lay, her blood still living,

[38563] a fillet-wreathed priestess doomed to go beneath the earth.

[38624] But now of lighter doings, though if another

[38670] had done the same, he would fall under the judge of morals;

[38731] for what was foul for the good Titius and Seius, became

[38788] a Crispinus: what can you do, when the man himself

[38840] is more loathsome than any crime? He bought a mullet

[38894] for six thousand—a sum that matched, indeed, six thousand

[38953] sesterces to its pounds, as those report who make great things greater.

[39026] I praise the schemer's cunning, if by so great a gift

[39081] he carried off the first place in some childless old man's will;

[39147] there is a deeper motive, if he sent it to a great mistress,

[39209] who is borne in a closed sedan with broad windows.

[39261] Expect nothing of the sort: he bought it for himself. Many things we see

[39335] which the poor and frugal Apicius never did. Was it you,

[39393] Crispinus, once girt with your native papyrus, who did this?

[39455] This price for scales? You might perhaps have bought the fisherman

[39523] for less than the fish; in the provinces an estate sells for as much,

[39594] but Apulia sells larger ones.

[39625] What banquets, then, do we suppose the Emperor himself

[39681] gulped down, when so many sesterces—a small part,

[39732] a trifle taken from the margin of a modest dinner—

[39784] were belched up by that purple buffoon of the great Palace,

[39845] now chief of the knights, who once with a great voice was wont

[39909] to hawk his fellow-townsmen's catfish from a broken stock?

[39969] Begin, Calliope. And you may sit—there is no need

[40020] for singing; a true affair is in hand. Tell the tale, maidens

[40083] of Pieria; may it profit me to have called you maidens.

[40140] When the last Flavian was tearing at a world half-dead

[40294] upholds, a wondrous bulk of Adriatic turbot,

[40860] not hesitating to call the fish a runaway,

[40904] long fattened in Caesar's preserves, and thence
