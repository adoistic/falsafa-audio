[49448] CHORUS So it's a fine thing not to sit beside Socrates and chatter, casting off music and neglecting the greatest things of the tragic art. And to idle away one's time on solemn-seeming words and the scratchings of trifles, is the part of a man out of his wits.

[49711] PLUTO Go now with joy, Aeschylus, on your way, and save our city with good counsels, and educate the senseless — there are many of them. And take this and carry it to Cleophon, and this to the revenue-collectors, Myrmex and Nicomachus together, and this to Archenomus; and tell them to come quickly to me here and not to delay; and if they don't come quickly, I, by Apollo, will brand them and tie them up together with Adeimantus, son of Leucolophus, and send them quickly underground.

[50199] AESCHYLUS I'll do this; and you hand over my seat to Sophocles to keep and preserve, in case I should ever come here again. For I judge him to be second in wisdom. And remember that the rascal, the false-speaker and the buffoon, never sits on my seat, not even against his will.

[50479] PLUTO Light him on his way, then, with the sacred torches, and escort him with his own songs and chants, singing aloud.

[50600] CHORUS First, good speed on the road give to the poet departing, rising into the light, you gods beneath the earth, and to the city good thoughts of great blessings. For so we'd be wholly freed from great griefs and from grievous armed encounters. And let Cleophon do the fighting, and any other of these who wishes, in his ancestral fields.
