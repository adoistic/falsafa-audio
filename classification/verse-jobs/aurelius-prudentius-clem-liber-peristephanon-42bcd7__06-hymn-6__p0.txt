WORK: Liber Peristephanon  (slug: aurelius-prudentius-clem-liber-peristephanon-42bcd7)

CHAPTER: 06-hymn-6



[0] 6. Hymn in Honour of the Most Blessed Martyrs Fructuosus, Bishop of the Church of Tarragona, and Augurius and Eulogius, Deacons.

[130] Happy Tarragona, Fructuosus, by your fires

[174] lifts up its head, glittering,

[206] shining from afar with two deacons.

[243] God looks kindly upon the Spaniards,

[281] since the powerful Trinity crowns

[316] the Spanish citadel with a threefold martyr.

[362] Burning Augurius seizes the heavens,

[400] and Eulogius too, at the same time, radiant,

[446] stretches toward the throne of Christ on high.

[494] Their leader, going before, and their master

[540] toward so great a glory, illustrious

[578] in his episcopal name, was Fructuosus,

[618] for, summoned suddenly by the governor’s

[660] command, the priest had come to the forum

[703] with his two deacons accompanying him.

[743] Then to the prison’s chain the executioner,

[788] fed on blood, was dragging the men;

[825] Fructuosus rejoices to run forward of his own will.

[878] And, lest any fear should strike his companions,

[928] the vehement teacher strengthens them as they go

[978] and kindles their faith with the warmth of Christ.

[1030] “Stand with me, men. The bloody serpent

[1071] calls God’s ministers to their punishment;

[1115] let not death frighten you; the palm is prepared.

[1166] Prison, for Christ’s worshippers, is a step to the crown,

[1225] prison carries them up to the heights of heaven,

[1275] prison reconciles God to the blessed.”

[1315] With these words they enter the cave of the accused,

[1369] they perform there the mystic washing,

[1409] and the darkness is amazed at the cleansing of the water.

[1468] Here for six continuous days they lie hidden;

[1515] at last they stand before the savage enemy’s tribunal:

[1571] the racks tremble at the three brothers.

[1613] The judge Aemilianus loomed over them,

[1653] fierce, turbulent, insolent, profane;

[1692] he ordered the demonic altars to be worshipped.

[1741] “You who, a teacher,” he says, “sow a new

[1784] kind of fiction, so that fickle girls

[1823] forsake the groves and abandon Jupiter,

[1864] condemn, if you are wise, this old wives’ dogma.

[1914] It is commanded by the mouth of Caesar Gallienus

[1964] that what the prince worships, all of us should worship.”

[2023] To him speaking thus the calm priest replies:

[2070] “I worship the eternal prince of days,

[2110] the maker and lord of Gallienus,

[2144] and Christ, sprung from the everlasting Father,

[2193] whose servant I am, and the shepherd of his flock.”

[2246] Smiling, that man says: “You were such once.”

[2293] Nor does his fury delay or curb its anger,

[2337] he dooms them to be burned in savage fires.

[2382] They exult and forbid the crowd to weep.

[2424] The priest sees some of the people

[2460] offering him a cup to sip:

[2488] “We are fasting,” he says; “I refuse the drink:

[2537] not yet does the ninth hour unseal the day;

[2582] never will I violate the dedicated law,

[2623] nor will death itself dissolve my holy rule.

[2669] So Christ, thirsting beneath the hour of the cross,

[2722] refusing the cup offered to him

[2755] and unwilling to taste, carried his thirst through.”

[2809] Meanwhile they enter a place shut in

[2847] by a rounded cage, soaked with the blood

[2889] of many beasts, which fury frequents,

[2928] when bloody spectacles resound

[2960] and a worthless gladiator, struck

[2995] by the hard sword, falls, and pleasure roars.

[3042] Here, with a blazing pyre, a black minister,

[3088] ordered to prepare the burning punishment,

[3132] built up the pyre with the final torches,

[3175] who, while he frees the bodies to be burned,

[3221] might release their souls, fervent with the love of light,

[3281] from the broken cave of the prison.

[3318] The pious companions vie in their services:

[3363] with the sandals loosed from his feet,

[3403] one, bending down, was eager to draw them off,

[3451] but holy Fructuosus forbids his bowed

[3490] face to be pressed upon: “Be gone,” he says,

[3536] “nor let your service burden my death.

[3576] Rather I myself will loosen my own feet,

[3618] lest my steps, hindered by the bonds,

[3657] rush into the fire with slow paces.

[3694] Why do laments wet your dripping cheeks?

[3736] Why do you ask that I be mindful of you?

[3778] For all the peoples I shall ask Christ.”

[3820] Scarcely had he said this when he himself loosens

[3871] the coverings of his feet, as Moses

[3908] once had done, drawing near to the bush.

[3950] It was not permitted to tread on the sacred burning

[4003] or to stand before God

[4027] before pure feet were set forward.

[4063] He stood with bare heel: lo, behold,

[4101] his spirit leaps up to heaven and sows a saying

[4150] which made all who heard it tremble:

[4188] “It is not, believe me, a punishment that you see,

[4240] which, hastened, passes in a thin moment,

[4283] nor does it snatch away life, but reshapes it.

[4331] Happy the souls to whom it falls

[4365] to climb on high to the Thunderer through fire,

[4414] whom one day the everlasting fire will flee.”

[4461] Amid these words they enter with swift steps

[4507] the crackling hearths, and threaten

[4544] the very trembling furnaces of the flames.

[4588] At last the bonds, which had drawn back their hands,

[4642] bound behind upon their backs,

[4674] fall away, burned, the skin untouched.

[4714] The punishment did not dare to hold back the palms,

[4767] to be raised to the Father in the manner of the cross;

[4823] it loosed the arms that they might pray to God.

[4872] You would think it the likeness of the three of old,

[4926] whom once, singing through the Babylonian fire,

[4975] the trembling tyrant beheld amazed.

[5012] But the pious flame then spared them,

[5051] the time of their passion not yet ripe,

[5092] and Christ not yet beginning the glory of death.

[5142] When the steaming heat shrank from these,

[5185] they pray that swift fire might fly to them

[5230] and give an end to their anxious perils.

[5272] Entreated, the Majesty at last bids

[5309] his servants to die, sent forth

[5342] from their perishing bodies and given back to himself.

[5398] An attendant from the governor’s house saw

[5442] heaven lie open to the martyrs

[5474] and the glorious men borne through the stars.

[5521] Indeed, warning his master’s little daughters,

[5569] he showed the mark of his father’s crime,

[5612] that those whom the forum slew live in heaven.

[5660] Then virginity was granted openly to see these things

[5715] through the clear sky, while the parent was blind,

[5767] that the house might fear its lord’s crime.

[5812] Then from the holy bodies the ashes

[5849] and the bones, drenched with wine, are gathered,

[5899] which in haste each one claimed for himself.

[5945] So great was the brethren’s love to carry home

[5993] the dedicated gifts of the holy ashes,

[6033] or to bear the faithful pledge in their bosom.

[6081] But lest they should divide among separate tombs,

[6132] in different places, the relics that must be raised again

[6191] and that soon will be with the Lord together,

[6238] they are seen, clad in snow-white robes,

[6280] commanding that the dust to be consecrated be restored

[6336] and shut up, mingled, in a hollow of marble.

[6382] O threefold honour, O three-formed summit,

[6426] by which the head of our city is exalted,

[6469] eminent above all the Spanish cities!

[6508] It is fitting to exult in three patrons,

[6550] by whose protection we are all cherished,

[6593] the peoples of the Pyrenean lands.

[6629] Let a choir of both sexes stand around;

[6670] hero, maiden, boy, old man, old woman,

[6710] sing duly the psalm of your Fructuosus.

[6751] Let the hymn echo, praising Augurius,

[6790] matching Eulogius with mingled measures;

[6832] let us render equal songs to equals.

[6870] Here let the gilded roofs sound in the citadel,

[6919] there let the soothing murmur of the shore arise,

[6970] and let the holiday seas compose a song.

[7012] One day there will be a time, as the world falls,

[7063] when Fructuosus, covering you, Tarragona,

[7106] from the fire, will free you from sharp torment.

[7156] Perhaps he will deign to give a remedy too

[7200] to my torments, with Christ’s favouring grace,

[7248] as he turns over these sweet hendecasyllables.
