WORK: Sāṃkhyatattvavivecana  (slug: simananda-samkhyatattvavivecana-69b94a)

CHAPTER: 04-the-self-spirit-purusa



[0] **Tats_3**

[12] [Base sūtra:] The {{puruṣa|Self}} [is the third great category].

[78] **Tats_3.c1**

[93] [Verse:] [Called puruṣa] from his lying within the city [of the body] (puri-śayana), from being the measure, from filling (pūraṇa), from abundant operation—he is beginningless, all-pervading, conscious, {{guṇa|constituent}}-less, supreme.

[333] **Tats_3.c2**

[348] [Verse:] [He is] the seer, the enjoyer, the {{kṣetrajña|knower of the field}}, stainless, of a non-productive nature, subtle, eternal, and indeed beginningless, without middle or end.

[533] **Tats_3.c3**

[548] [Verse:] "All is pervaded by him"—therefore he is all-pervading; and because he possesses the experience of pleasure he is conscious, and likewise constituent-less.

[714] **Tats_3.c4**

[729] [Verse:] Through the absence of the constituents sattva and the rest, he is "supreme," remembered by [the word] "supremacy." Through connection with visible prakṛti he is the seer; through experiencing he is the enjoyer.

[951] **Tats_3.c5**

[966] [Verse:] In the field he knows what pertains to the constituents; hence he is proclaimed "{{kṣetrajña|knower of the field}}." Through the absence of contact with the auspicious, inauspicious, and so on, he is held to be stainless.

[1198] **Tats_3.c6**

[1213] [Verse:] Because he is seedless and does not bring forth [offspring], he is of a non-productive nature. Through being part-less and supra-sensible he is subtle; eternal, because he is everlasting.

[1411] **Tats_3.c7**

[1426] [Verse:] Thus the Sāṃkhya {{puruṣa|Self}} has been expounded by the ancient sages. Living being (jīva), creature, person, self, puruṣa, worshipper, man,

[1580] **Tats_3.c8**

[1595] [Verse:] field-knower, imperishable, life-breath, "who-is-this?", the knower, the unborn—these are the names, in the Sāṃkhya, of what is termed the puruṣa.

[1752] **Tats_3.c9**

[1767] So [it is said]: "He who knows the twenty-five principles, in whatever stage of life he abides—shaven, with matted locks, or wearing a topknot—is liberated; herein is no doubt."

[1946] **Tats_3.c10**

[1962] [Verse:] Is this {{puruṣa|Self}}, who is beyond prakṛti, an agent or a non-agent? If he were the agent of auspicious and inauspicious [deeds], he would do them without the three functions [of the guṇas]. [And] if agency belongs to the constituents seen in the world, how can [the puruṣa] be the attribute-bearer [of agency]?

[2288] **Tats_3.c11**

[2304] [Verse:] Conformity [to discipline] arising from restraint, observance, [self-]sprinkling, and right reckoning; in the manifesting of the transformation [that is] knowledge and lordliness—this is the sāttvika function.

[2524] **Tats_3.c12**

[2540] [Verse:] Passion, anger, greed, slandering others, censure, [self-]satisfaction, harshness whether provoked or unprovoked—this is well known as the rajas-intellect.

[2706] **Tats_3.c13**

[2722] [Verse:] Madness, intoxication, dejection, godlessness, addiction to women, sleep, sloth, cruelty, and impurity—this is the tāmasa function.

[2864] **Tats_3.c14**

[2880] [Verse:] Having seen this triad of functions, [we conclude that] agency belongs to the constituents; and [hence] the puruṣa is established as a non-agent—this is held to be the [Sāṃkhya] settled doctrine ({{vivekajñāna|established conclusion}}).

[3127] **Tats_3.c15**

[3143] [Verse:] Through the distorted perception [born] of rajas and tamas, the unwise one—veiling his own self—holds these constituents of operative prakṛti [to be his own], [thinking] "I do this for myself," and so despises [the truth].

[3376] **Tats_3.c16**

[3392] [Verse:] Incapable of [so much as] containing the tip of a hair within his belly, yet he thought, "all this was done by me"; he is to be regarded like a madman—such is [his] ignorance. O Fate! who [is more deluded] than one who fancies himself learned?

[3646] **Tats_3.c17**

[3662] This is stated in the Gītā [3.27]: "Actions are in every way performed by the constituents of prakṛti; [yet] one whose self is deluded by the I-maker thinks, 'I am the agent.'"

[3840] **Tats_3.c18**

[3856] [Gītā 13.31:] "Because he is beginningless and constituent-less, this imperishable supreme Self, though dwelling in the body, O son of Kuntī, neither acts nor is tainted."

[4029] **Tats_3.c19**

[4045] [Gītā 13.29:] "And he who always sees that actions are in every way performed by prakṛti alone, [and sees] the Self as a non-agent—he [truly] sees"—and so forth.

[4208] **Tats_3.c20**

[4224] [Verse:] Now consider whether the {{puruṣa|Self}} is one or many. From the diversity of [people's] mixed pleasure, pain, and delusion, of [their] pure births and deaths, the plurality of puruṣas is established; and [also] from the distinction of worlds, stages of life, and social orders.

[4514] **Tats_3.c21**

[4530] [Verse:] If the puruṣa were one, a collapse of intelligence would follow: when one was happy, all in turn would have to be happy; when one was in pain, all would have to be in pain.

[4713] **Tats_3.c22**

[4729] [Verse:] Therefore plurality is well established; many puruṣas are remembered. From the diversity of configuration, womb, disposition, association, and bodily division—[i.e.] from the plurality of subtle bodies ({{liṅgaśarīra|transmigrating body}})—the Sāṃkhya teachers, beginning with Kapila, Āsuri, Pañcaśikha, and Patañjali, describe the plurality of puruṣas.

[5093] **Tats_3.c23**

[5109] The teachers who uphold the Veda—Hari, Hara, Hiraṇyagarbha, Vyāsa, and the rest—[hold] the Self to be one; and so the śruti [says]: "The {{puruṣa|Cosmic Person}} alone is all this, what has been and what is to be; he is also the lord of immortality, [the one] who grows beyond [all] through [other] nourishment. That alone is fire, that the sun, that the wind, and that the moon."

[5491] **Tats_3.c24**

[5507] [Śruti:] "That alone is the bright [seed], that is brahman, those are the waters, he is Prajāpati; that alone is the truth and the immortal, he is liberation, that is the supreme goal."

[5694] **Tats_3.c25**

[5710] [Śruti:] "That is the imperishable, that is the adorable [splendour] of Savitṛ, than which there is nothing higher nor other; than which there is nothing subtler nor greater."

[5887] **Tats_3.c26**

[5903] [Śruti:] "Like a tree, [or] like a pillar, the One stands fixed in heaven; by that {{puruṣa|Person}} this [all] is filled. On every side [it has] hands and feet, on every side eyes, heads, and faces."

[6105] **Tats_3.c27**

[6121] [Śruti:] "On every side having ears in the world, it abides enveloping all; appearing through the qualities of all the senses, [yet] devoid of all the senses."

[6282] **Tats_3.c28**

[6298] [Śruti:] "The lord of every creature, the great refuge of all; on every side [are] all beings—[it is] the Self of all, the source of all."

[6438] **Tats_3.c29**

[6454] [Śruti:] "That in which all dissolves, the sages know as brahman; for the one Self of beings alone is established in each and every being,

[6594] **Tats_3.c30**

[6610] [Śruti:] "and is seen as one and as manifold, like the moon [reflected] in water; for that one great Self dwells in all beings, moving and unmoving, by which all this is pervaded"—and so forth.
